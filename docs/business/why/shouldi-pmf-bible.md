# ShouldI — PMF Strategy Bible

> **Core Positioning**
>
> We help you make the next important decision with confidence.
> Every decision makes your next one better.

---

## 1. Why This Document Exists

This document is the PMF version of the strategy bible.

It is built for the next 90 days, not the next 10 years.

This document keeps the long-term vision while removing PMF distractions.

---

## 2. Rule Zero

People come for clarity.

They stay for better judgment.

---

## 3. Mission (PMF Framing)

Help people make better decisions today, so they make better decisions for life.

---

## 4. Vision (Unchanged)

Build the Judgment Intelligence layer for human decisions.

We are not changing the destination.

We are changing the go-to-market entry point.

---

## 5. Immediate Value Principle

Every session must deliver value today.

Long-term compounding is a bonus, never a prerequisite.

---

## 6. Time-to-Value Standard

A new user must understand why ShouldI is different within one completed decision.

If value requires multiple decisions and delayed follow-up, PMF risk is high.

---

## 7. Primary User Job

"I have an important decision. Help me choose with confidence."

Not:

"Train me to become better at judgment."

---

## 8. Product System Hierarchy (PMF Core)

Explore -> Decide -> Outcome Replay -> DNA

Outcome Replay is a core product surface, not a supporting feature.

Judgment remains the compounding output, not the acquisition hook.

---

## 9. Company Truth

ShouldI never sells judgment.

ShouldI sells clarity.

Judgment is the long-term consequence.

Clarity creates action. Action creates learning. Learning compounds into judgment.

### The Product Equation

Clarity -> Confidence -> Action -> Outcome -> Outcome Replay -> Calibration -> Judgment

If a feature does not strengthen this chain, it is probably not a ShouldI feature.

---

## 10. Decision Quality

A better decision is not one with a better outcome.

A better decision is one that:

- considers the right tradeoffs,
- acknowledges uncertainty,
- acts on the best available evidence,
- and remains defensible even if the outcome is unfavorable.

ShouldI optimizes decision quality, not outcome certainty.

---

## 11. Confidence Loop

People do not buy recommendations.

People buy confidence to act.

Therefore every completed decision should increase at least one of:

- Decision clarity
- Action confidence
- Commitment to execution

If confidence does not increase, the session has failed.

---

## 12. PMF Principle #1: Deliver Immediate Clarity

Users come with an urgent decision, not a learning intention.

Every first session must end with a clear recommendation and next action.

No onboarding flow should require explicit "judgment training" before useful output.

---

## 13. PMF Principle #2: Increase Decision Frequency

Major life/career decisions are infrequent.

The product must create a frequency ladder:

- Daily: Explore decision feed
- Weekly: Decision Gym
- Monthly: high-stakes personal decisions

---

## 14. PMF Principle #3: Match Pricing to Importance Moments

Pricing must follow decision intensity, not calendar time.

Free -> Credits -> Subscription

Users pay for depth at decision moments.

Subscription unlocks continuity and compounding context.

---

## 15. PMF Principle #4: Shorten Time-to-Value

Decision DNA is powerful long-term but weak as first-session value.

Generate an immediate **Decision Lens** after the first meaningful decision, with transparent confidence levels.

A user should feel unique value in one completed decision.

---

## 16. PMF Principle #5: Compound Through Outcome Replay and Calibration

Reasoning quality alone can be replicated.

Compounding behavior data cannot.

Build moat on longitudinal user decision data:

Decision Record -> Outcome -> Outcome Replay -> Calibration -> DNA

---

## 17. PMF Principle #6: Quality Before Community Scale

UGC-first strategy usually leads to low quality at launch.

Year 1 content should be AI-curated + human-edited.

Users vote and interact before they are asked to create content.

---

## 18. PMF Principle #7: Make Outcome Return Valuable

If users do not return for outcomes, compounding fails.

Outcome follow-up must feel personally valuable, not administrative.

---

## 19. Product North Star

Maximize meaningful decisions completed with confidence.

---

## 20. Metric Stack

### Primary

Meaningful Decisions Completed

### Secondary

Returned for Outcome Update

### Ultimate

User confidence before action increases over time.

---

## 21. PMF Guardrail Questions

Every roadmap item must pass:

1. Does this improve first-decision value?
2. Does this improve confidence-to-action conversion?
3. Does this increase outcome return probability?
4. Can this be measured inside 90 days?

If no, archive for later.

### The ShouldI Test

Every feature must answer:

Will this help someone make a meaningful decision they otherwise would not have made?

If not, it is probably not ShouldI.

---

## 22. Product Surface Strategy

### Explore

Daily decision habit loop (not community vanity loop).

### Decide

Immediate high-stakes decision clarity.

### Outcome Replay

Prediction vs Reality with explicit calibration.

### DNA

Compounding profile derived from repeated decisions and outcomes.

---

## 23. Explore Repositioning

Explore is a Daily Decision Feed.

Community is an implementation method, not the strategic objective.

Objective is repeat frequency and interaction quality.

---

## 24. Decision Gym (Weekly Layer)

Short weekly scenarios train tradeoff thinking and calibration.

It bridges low-frequency life decisions with medium-frequency engagement.

---

## 25. Learning Loop (Core Flywheel)

Decision -> Prediction -> Reality -> Outcome Replay -> Calibration -> DNA Update

Outcome Replay is the experience surface.

Calibration is the growth mechanism.

---

## 26. Decision DNA

DNA is not the hero at first touch.

DNA is the compounding layer unlocked by repeated, high-quality cycles.

---

## 27. First-Session Output Contract

After one serious decision, every user should receive:

- Decision recommendation with rationale
- Key tradeoffs made explicit
- Confidence framing
- Initial Decision Lens (probabilistic, transparent confidence)

---

## 28. Confidence as Product Currency

We optimize for confident action, not maximal token output.

Clear recommendation quality and user confidence are more important than verbose analysis.

---

## 29. Less Thinking. More Deciding.

ShouldI exists to reduce hesitation, not increase analysis.

Complexity should be hidden from users whenever possible.

More confidence. Less regret.

---

## 30. Recommendation UX Principle

Do not end at analysis.

Always close with:

- Recommended path
- Why this path
- What to monitor next

---

## 31. Outcome Capture Principle

Outcome updates should use specific, memory-based prompts.

Example pattern:

"Three months ago you predicted X at 80%. What happened?"

---

## 32. Data Moat Architecture

Core moat assets are:

- Structured decision records
- Time-linked outcomes
- Prediction/reality gaps
- Personalized calibration history
- Evolving Decision DNA

This moat is lifecycle data, not prompt engineering.

---

## 33. Monetization Principles

Users should pay for important decisions, not for idle calendar time.

Credits unlock depth.

Subscriptions unlock continuity.

Both models coexist.

---

## 34. Pricing Architecture (Working Model)

- Free: Basic decision support
- Council Credits: paid deep dives per important decision
- Continuity Subscription: memory/outcome replay/calibration continuity layer

Exact price points are experiment variables, not strategy constants.

---

## 35. Go-to-Market Wedge

Start with one explicit wedge:

Professionals making career decisions.

First user cohort: software engineers making offer, switch, and compensation decisions.

This cohort has high AI adoption, strong distribution surfaces, and high shareability.

Expand adjacencies only after repeatable conversion and retention signals in this wedge.

---

## 36. B2B2C Channel Hypothesis

Potential distribution partners:

- Career platforms
- Universities and bootcamps
- Talent ecosystems

Thesis: partners sell better outcomes; ShouldI powers decision infrastructure.

---

## 37. What We De-prioritize Until PMF

Archive from current 90-day execution focus:

- Digital Judgment Twin
- Enterprise-heavy platform layers
- Team rooms and executive dashboards
- Long-horizon infrastructure narratives

These are post-PMF expansion topics.

---

## 38. Operating Principle for Focus

We do not optimize for conceptual completeness.

We optimize for measurable PMF progress.

### AI Principle

Models generate.

Frameworks structure.

Outcome Replay compounds.

Humans decide.

### Permanent Constraints

We never optimize for more thinking when more clarity is possible.

We never delay value to demonstrate intelligence.

We never mistake longer reasoning for better decisions.

We never optimize engagement over outcomes.

---

## 39. 90-Day Product Priorities

1. Improve first-decision confidence outcome
2. Launch Outcome Replay loop baseline
3. Increase outcome return rate
4. Validate credits-driven monetization behavior
5. Establish daily/weekly engagement cadence via Explore + Gym

---

## 40. 90-Day Validation Targets (Template)

- First-session "this is different from generic AI" rate
- Decision-to-action conversion rate
- Outcome return within expected window
- Credit purchase rate on high-stakes decisions
- Repeat decision completion rate

Targets should be filled by owner team with baseline and thresholds.

## 43. Founder Rule

Every completed decision should produce an artifact worth revisiting.

This artifact starts as a Decision Record and later powers Outcome Replay, sharing, calibration, and DNA updates.

---

## 44. One-Sentence Strategic Core

People do not come to ShouldI to become wiser.

They come to solve an important decision.

They stay because every decision makes them wiser.

---

## 45. Final Ending Line

We do not measure success by the number of decisions AI makes.

We measure success by the number of better decisions people make.

ShouldI wins when users stop asking us the same question twice.

The best decision assistant is the one users eventually outgrow.
