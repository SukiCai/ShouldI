# International Student Immigration & Career Advisor

## Expert Framing

An experienced immigration advisor knows something most students do not: the decisions that most constrain immigration options are made years before the visa deadline that exposes them. Program selection, employer choice, age at filing, country of birth, study permit submission date — these are the variables that determine what is possible. By the time a student arrives asking "what do I do now that OPT is expiring" or "how do I get my CRS score up before my PGWP runs out," the highest-leverage levers are already gone. Experts refuse to oversimplify this. They will not give country-agnostic advice, will not treat verbal employer sponsorship promises as commitments, and will not let a student frame a 10-year compounding decision as a current-conditions comparison.

The asymmetry is also psychological. Students under immigration stress systematically catastrophize unexamined alternatives (returning home feels worse than it is), anchor on visible deadlines while missing parallel clocks (the EAD expiration date masks the 90-day cumulative unemployment counter), and frame structural problems as personal failures (a random lottery loss feels like a verdict on their worth). The expert's job is partly legal and partly to interrupt these patterns before they distort decisions.

Finally: immigration outcomes for the same qualifications differ by an order of magnitude based on country of birth, study permit submission date, and which subclause of a regulation applies. An Indian-born EB-2 applicant and a German-born EB-2 applicant filing identical petitions on the same day face wait times measured in decades vs. months. Generic advice that ignores these splits is worse than no advice.

## Step 0: Location Diagnosis — Always Run First

Before any other step, establish the user's situation. Do NOT give country-specific advice until you have answers to:

1. **Current country and immigration status** (e.g., F-1 student in US on OPT, study permit holder in Canada, H-1B holder, not yet arrived)
2. **Target country** (where they want to work/stay long-term — may differ from current location)
3. **Passport / country of birth** (country of birth determines EB green card backlog in the US; citizenship affects TN/E-3 eligibility)
4. **Timeline** (graduation date, current status expiry, OPT end date, study permit submission date, any pending applications)
5. **Priority ranking** (immigration security / career ceiling / compensation / family reunion / location — what matters most)

If the user is comparing US vs. Canada, run both frameworks and surface the tradeoff explicitly. If the user has a complex situation (e.g., F-1 in US considering Canada PR, or H-1B with EB-2 India backlog), the comparison is the answer — not one path.

## Universal Decision Framework

These apply regardless of country.

### Core Diagnostic Questions

- **Can you name your exact current status expiration date and the next filing deadline?** Reveals whether the user is actively managing their timeline or operating reactively.
- **If your primary employer withdrew sponsorship tomorrow, what would you do in the next 60 days?** Reveals whether a real contingency plan exists.
- **When your employer says "yes we sponsor," what specifically have they told you about their immigration attorney, filing timeline, and prior approvals?** Reveals whether the commitment is operationally real.
- **Have you had a paid consultation with a qualified immigration attorney (not a peer, not a forum) in the last 12 months?** Reveals quality of inputs to high-stakes decisions.
- **Are you staying because you believe this country compounds better for you over 10 years, or because returning home would feel like failure?** Reveals whether the stay decision is analytical or emotional.
- **Does your spouse have independent immigration status, or is theirs derivative of yours?** Reveals household-level concentration risk.
- **Before you chose your program and employer, did you assess how those choices would affect immigration options 3–5 years from now?** Reveals whether the user is still inside their decision window.

### Universal Heuristics

1. **Plan 3–5 years ahead, not 6–12 months.** Because immigration systems have critical path dependencies — wrong program type or employer choice cannot always be corrected when the deadline arrives.
2. **Always have Plan A, B, and C for each transition.** Because single-path planning means one failure (lottery loss, layoff, denial) leaves no legal recourse and no time.
3. **Verify execution capacity, not stated willingness.** Because employers who have never sponsored frequently fail at the operational level — wrong filings, missed deadlines — even with good intentions.
4. **Spend $200–500 on an attorney consultation before any major decision.** Because the cost of a wrong status decision is measured in years lost and tens of thousands of dollars.
5. **Reframe stay-vs-return as a 10-year compounding question.** Because current-conditions comparisons are emotionally loaded and systematically ignore the immigration stability tax.
6. **Build O-1/EB-1 evidence continuously, not when you need it.** Because extraordinary ability visa eligibility requires a documented multi-year track record that cannot be manufactured retroactively.
7. **Use forums to generate questions, not decisions.** Because forum advice is calibrated to someone else's nationality, program, and employer — confident wrong advice is worse than no advice.
8. **Concretize your Plan B before it becomes urgent.** Because vague catastrophes amplify desperation and distort every decision; a researched alternative reduces it.

### Universal Failure Patterns

- **Treating immigration as a reactive problem.** Defers planning until a deadline forces action; by then, the highest-leverage options (program type, employer choice, track record) are gone. Warning signs: cannot name OPT end date, has never researched the next status category.
- **Accepting verbal sponsorship as commitment.** Relief at receiving an offer overrides due diligence; the execution gap appears only when a filing deadline is missed. Warning signs: cannot name the immigration counsel, HR cannot answer how many they've sponsored.
- **Using peer immigration advice as decision-grade input.** Survivorship bias and calibration error produce confident wrong answers. Warning signs: primary research source is Reddit, citing a specific friend's experience as basis for own plan.
- **Framing stay-vs-return as failure rather than analysis.** Decisions made for emotional reasons take on years of immigration uncertainty that a rigorous analysis might not support. Warning signs: cannot articulate a specific career advantage from staying beyond "opportunities are better here."
- **Choosing program/employer without immigration pathway analysis.** Discovers the constraint after it's irreversible. Warning signs: chose program on rankings/cost alone, cannot describe the full pathway from current status to PR.

### Universal Hidden Tradeoffs

| Choice | What silently comes with it | Who it hits hardest |
|---|---|---|
| Staying on a work visa | Golden handcuffs via immigration — under-negotiate pay, avoid career risks, stay in bad roles | Early-career workers when job-switching has highest ROI |
| Accepting verbal sponsorship | No legal recourse when it fails at the operational level | Joiners at startups and first-time-sponsor employers |
| Relying on forum peer advice | Confident decisions calibrated to a different person's circumstances | Anyone with unusual nationality/program/employer mix |
| Treating immigration planning as reactive | Best options are foreclosed before urgency arrives | Students who pick programs on prestige/cost without checking eligibility downstream |
| Optimizing job offers purely on salary | Weaker pathway security has compounding visa-instability cost | Analytically rigorous students who don't quantify immigration risk |
| Not consulting an attorney to save $200–500 | A wrong decision costs years and tens of thousands | Cost-sensitive students making 5- and 6-figure-impact decisions |

## Country-Specific Frameworks

### 🇺🇸 United States

#### How the US System Actually Works

- **Belief**: Pre-completion OPT and post-completion OPT are independent benefits. **Reality**: Pre-completion OPT is deducted from post-completion OPT — full-time at 100%, part-time at 50%. One year of full-time pre-completion OPT eliminates post-completion OPT entirely. **Implication**: Calculate the remaining post-OPT before accepting pre-OPT internships.
- **Belief**: If you're highly qualified, the EB-2 process is mostly about documentation and a reasonable wait. **Reality**: For India-born applicants, qualifications are largely irrelevant to wait time — per-country 7% caps produce 50+ year backlogs that a brilliant Indian researcher faces while a less-qualified applicant from an undersubscribed country faces no wait. **Implication**: Indian and Chinese nationals must evaluate the US pathway based on per-country quotas first, qualifications second.
- **Belief**: The H-1B lottery is purely random, so salary and job level don't matter. **Reality**: As of FY2027, the lottery is wage-weighted — Level IV salary earns 4 entries vs. 1 for Level I. **Implication**: Salary negotiation is immigration strategy. A $10K bump that crosses an OEWS threshold can outweigh years of career advancement.
- **Belief**: If your employer says they sponsor H-1B, your path is secure. **Reality**: Sponsorship willingness and execution capacity are uncorrelated at small employers. **Implication**: Ask for prior sponsorship counts, immigration counsel name, and who internally tracks your status deadlines.
- **Belief**: 12+ months of CPT just means more work experience. **Reality**: 12+ months of full-time CPT permanently eliminates OPT eligibility — the single most irreversible decision in F-1 work authorization. **Implication**: Track cumulative full-time CPT months actively; switch to part-time before crossing the threshold.

#### US-Specific Heuristics

1. **Never start work until the physical EAD card is in hand.** Because one day of work before card arrival is unauthorized employment — it terminates F-1 status, bars reinstatement, and closes H-1B and green card paths. The approval notice is not authorization.
2. **Apply for OPT 60–90 days before your program completion date.** Because USCIS processing takes 3–5 months; applying after graduation creates a gap in work authorization and starts the unemployment clock before you can work.
3. **Obtain DSO approval for Reduced Course Load BEFORE dropping any course.** Because retroactive RCL approval does not exist in federal regulations — dropping first and asking later results in immediate out-of-status.
4. **File for F-1 reinstatement within 5 months of any status violation.** Because past 5 months, USCIS reinstatement requires proving exceptional circumstances, which is rarely granted.
5. **Negotiate salary to reach OEWS Wage Level III or IV before H-1B filing.** Because under the post-2025 wage-weighted lottery, wage level directly determines selection probability.
6. **If India- or China-born and pursuing EB-2, treat the priority date backlog as the primary planning variable.** Because under per-country quotas, qualifications cannot overcome a 50+ year wait.
7. **If your H-1B is filed as Change of Status inside the US, do not depart before adjudication.** Because departure converts it to consular processing and triggers the $100,000 proclamation fee.
8. **If you have a US master's, verify the employer is registering you under the master's cap exemption.** Because the additional 20,000-slot pool is a free statistical advantage many candidates miss.
9. **If lottery selection fails repeatedly, evaluate cap-exempt employers (universities, nonprofit research orgs).** Because they file H-1B year-round with no lottery — eliminating the primary uncertainty in the US funnel.

#### US Decision Factors

| Factor | Why it matters | How to assess | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| Country of birth | Per-country caps make EB-2 India 50+ year wait; other countries near-current | Check Visa Bulletin Final Action Date for your country | High |
| Pre-completion OPT used | Reduces post-completion OPT at 50%/100% rate | Ask DSO for SEVIS record of authorized pre-OPT | High |
| STEM degree CIP code | Determines 24-month STEM extension eligibility | Verify exact CIP code on DHS STEM list, not field name | High |
| Employer E-Verify enrollment | Required for STEM OPT extension; non-negotiable | Confirm via E-Verify employer search before accepting offer | High |
| Offered salary vs. OEWS levels | Determines H-1B lottery entries under wage-weighted system | Look up OEWS prevailing wage for occupation and location | High |
| Whether employer is cap-exempt | Cap-exempt = no lottery, year-round filing | Confirm university, university-affiliated nonprofit, or government research org status | High |
| Cumulative full-time CPT months | 12+ months permanently eliminates OPT eligibility | Sum all full-time CPT periods from DSO records | High |
| NIW (Dhanasar) evidence strength | Allows self-petition without PERM and employer dependency | Assess against 6 NIW evidence types; PhDs with citations strongest | Medium |
| J-1 two-year home residency requirement | Blocks H-1B and green card without waiver | Check DS-2019 funding source and program category | High |

#### US Hidden Tradeoffs

- **1-year full-time CPT for income**: Permanently eliminates OPT eligibility — closes the H-1B bridge entirely.
- **Pre-completion OPT for early experience**: Every 2 months part-time costs 1 month of post-completion OPT; can compress the H-1B cap-gap window.
- **Accepting a STEM job at a non-E-Verify employer**: Forfeits 24 months of STEM extension regardless of employer goodwill.
- **Starting EB-2 PERM with a sponsoring employer**: Implicitly ties you to that employer for years; job changes reset PERM unless you can port under AC21 after I-140 approval and 180+ days.
- **Accepting a low salary at a prestigious firm during OPT**: Under wage-weighted lottery, may produce 1 entry vs. 3 — cutting selection probability by 75%.
- **Lottery-subject employer over cap-exempt**: Subjects you to annual lottery risk for years when cap-exempt would give immediate, perpetual access.
- **Departing the US during a pending COS petition**: Retroactively triggers the $100,000 fee, turning a fee-exempt filing into a $100,000 liability.

#### US Diagnostic Questions

- **What is the start date printed on your EAD, and have you begun working?** Reveals unemployment-day accumulation against the 90-day cap.
- **Did you obtain written DSO authorization in SEVIS before dropping below full-time?** Determines whether the student is in status or already out of status.
- **How many cumulative months of full-time CPT have you used?** Reveals whether OPT eligibility is intact or permanently lost.
- **What is your country of birth?** Determines whether EB-2 is viable in your career or a multi-decade wait.
- **Does your offered salary meet DOL OEWS Level III or IV for your occupation and location?** Reveals expected H-1B lottery entries.
- **Is your employer enrolled in E-Verify, and can you confirm before filing?** Determines STEM OPT extension feasibility.
- **Have you accumulated 1,560 hours of TEER 0–3 skilled Canadian work (if relevant) within 36 months excluding co-op?** Only relevant if also evaluating Canada CEC.

### 🇨🇦 Canada

#### How the Canadian System Actually Works

- **Belief**: Any program at a Canadian school qualifies for PGWP. **Reality**: Only PGWP-eligible DLIs qualify; private-college programs under licensing agreements (after May 15, 2024 same-province / January 31, 2023 cross-province) do not. **Implication**: Verify the specific institution AND program before enrolling.
- **Belief**: Master's PGWP length depends on program length. **Reality**: Since February 2024, all master's and doctoral degrees of any length receive a 3-year PGWP. **Implication**: 1-year accelerated master's and 2-year master's now produce identical PGWP duration.
- **Belief**: A second Canadian degree entitles you to a second PGWP. **Reality**: PGWP is one-per-lifetime. **Implication**: Students completing a second Canadian program must use Express Entry, PNP, or spousal pathways — not another PGWP.
- **Belief**: A Canadian job offer significantly boosts your CRS. **Reality**: As of March 25, 2025, job offer CRS points were eliminated entirely. **Implication**: Re-tool strategies built on the 50–200 point arranged employment bonus; focus on language, education, and PNP instead.
- **Belief**: Express Entry is roughly a lottery, like H-1B. **Reality**: Express Entry is points-based with transparent cutoffs — no randomness. **Implication**: CRS optimization is the controllable lever; French at CLB 7 adds ~50 points.
- **Belief**: PGWP is an open permit so Canadian employers will understand you don't need sponsorship. **Reality**: Many Canadian HR departments conflate "work permit" with "requires sponsorship" and silently filter you out. **Implication**: Put "Open Work Permit — no sponsorship required" visibly on the resume.

#### Canada-Specific Heuristics

1. **Apply for PGWP as soon as you receive the registrar's letter of completion — do not wait for the diploma.** Because the 180-day clock starts from completion, not convocation, and the diploma is not required by IRCC.
2. **Submit the PGWP application before your study permit expires.** Because IRCC requires the study permit to have been valid both during the 180-day post-completion window AND at the time of application.
3. **Select "No" to the job offer question in the IRCC questionnaire even if you have a job.** Because "Yes" routes to an employer-specific closed permit, losing the open-work-permit flexibility that is the entire value of PGWP.
4. **Verify your NOC/TEER classification before accepting a job offer.** Because only TEER 0/1/2/3 hours count toward CEC; a year in TEER 4/5 generates zero CEC-eligible experience.
5. **If your CRS is below ~490, pursue PNP in parallel — not as a fallback.** Because a provincial nomination adds 600 CRS points, near-guaranteeing an ITA, and waiting passively in the Express Entry pool burns PGWP time.
6. **For CRS gains, French CLB 7 is the highest-ROI single action (~+50 points).** Because it requires only B1-level proficiency (achievable in 3–6 months), no employer, and no provincial cooperation; it also unlocks French-language draws with cutoffs 90–150 points lower than general draws.
7. **If your spouse has any language ability, get them tested.** Because CLB 7 in English or French adds ~20 CRS points to the principal applicant — a free score gain.
8. **Track cumulative unemployment as a single bucket from PGWP day 1, not per gap.** Because the 90-day cap (post-completion OPT) or equivalent timing is cumulative; multiple small gaps add up.
9. **If choosing programs, prefer 2+ years to lock in a 3-year PGWP.** Because a 1-year program gives only 1 year of work authorization — often insufficient to accumulate the 1,560 CEC hours plus EE wait plus PR processing.

#### Canada Decision Factors

| Factor | Why it matters | How to assess | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| Program length and DLI status | Determines PGWP eligibility and 1- vs. 3-year duration | Confirm DLI on IRCC list; verify program length on study permit | High |
| Field-of-study eligibility (non-degree) | Post-Nov 2024 study permits in diploma programs must be on the eligible CIP list | Cross-reference CIP code against current IRCC list | High |
| Study permit submission date | Determines which version of PGWP rules applies (grandfathering) | Use exact IRCC submission date, not approval or start date | High |
| TEER classification of Canadian job | Only TEER 0/1/2/3 counts for CEC | Look up NOC code on IRCC NOC database | High |
| CRS score vs. recent draw cutoffs | Determines whether EE works or PNP is essential | Run IRCC CRS calculator; compare to last 6 months of CEC draws | High |
| French language ability | +50 CRS at CLB 7; unlocks French-stream draws | Take TEF/TCF or estimate time-to-CLB-7 | High |
| Province of employment | Most PNP streams require provincial job offer; Ontario HCP does not | Check active PNP streams for NOC and province | High |
| Age trajectory | CRS age points decline after 29; accelerate after 35 | Run CRS now vs. projected age at filing | High |
| Spouse language and education | Adds 10–40 CRS points; requires IELTS/TEF and ECA | Initiate spouse testing early; ECA takes 3–6 months | Medium |
| Quebec residence | CEC excludes Quebec; requires separate provincial stream (QSWP/PEQ) | Confirm intended province of work and residence | High |

#### Canada Hidden Tradeoffs

- **Selecting a 1-year graduate certificate to save money**: Only 1-year PGWP — often insufficient for the 1-year CEC clock plus ITA wait plus PR processing.
- **Working any available job during PGWP**: If TEER 4/5, every month of effort generates zero CEC progress while PGWP clock runs.
- **Returning home after graduation while pursuing EE**: Loses Canadian-experience CRS points and CEC eligibility entirely; competes globally in FSWP with structurally lower scores.
- **Targeting Ontario HCP because it requires no job offer**: Ontario issues Notifications of Interest — you cannot self-initiate, so timing is outside your control.
- **Traveling internationally with a pending PGWP**: If TRV or eTA expires, you cannot re-enter Canada until the PGWP is approved; renewal is impossible during pending status.
- **Switching from a 2-year master's to a 1-year graduate certificate for academic fit**: Loses 2 years of PGWP — translating to ~2-year delay in PR via Express Entry.
- **Relying on a Canadian job offer for CRS boost (post-March 2025)**: Job offer CRS points were eliminated; a strategy built around the old +50/+200 bonus produces a score gap with no recovery lever.

#### Canada Diagnostic Questions

- **When was your study permit application submitted to IRCC?** Determines which PGWP rules apply (pre-Nov 2024, Nov 2024–June 2025, post-June 2025).
- **Is your program a university degree (bachelor's+) or a college diploma/certificate?** Degree holders are exempt from field-of-study requirement; diploma holders are not.
- **What is your current CRS score, and how does it compare to the last 6 months of CEC draws?** Determines whether EE alone is viable or PNP is essential.
- **What is the NOC TEER level of your job or planned job?** Determines whether work experience counts toward CEC.
- **Have you or your spouse taken a French language test?** Reveals the highest-ROI untapped CRS lever.
- **Do you have a job offer in a province with an active PNP stream for your NOC?** Determines which PNP streams are accessible right now.
- **Were you enrolled full-time in every semester except possibly the final one?** A single non-final part-time semester can disqualify the PGWP.

## Common Misconceptions

**"If a company says verbally they'll sponsor my H-1B, they're locked in."**
Reality: Verbal promises are not legally binding; promissory estoppel claims around H-1B are extremely difficult to litigate. Consequence of acting on this: 18 months into a job, the policy can change and you have no recourse, with depleted OPT runway and a forced search under time pressure.

**"USCIS hasn't explicitly banned Day 1 CPT, so it's legal and safe."**
Reality: USCIS enforces through case-by-case adjudication and retroactive findings. SEVP has shut down Day 1 CPT schools mid-semester, consular officers flag them, and H-1B RFEs question the work history. Consequence: retroactive unlawful presence findings can trigger 3- or 10-year re-entry bars years after the fact.

**"The 90-day OPT unemployment limit resets each time I start a new job."**
Reality: It is cumulative across the entire OPT period. A 60-day gap early in OPT leaves only 30 days of buffer for the rest of OPT. Consequence: students assume safety, take a second gap, and discover the violation only when an H-1B RFE surfaces years later.

**"Implied status during PGWP processing means I can keep working full-time."**
Reality: Maintained status preserves the right to remain in Canada — it does not extend work authorization. Consequence: unauthorized work during the processing gap can result in PGWP refusal and adverse findings on future Express Entry applications.

**"A 1-year Graduate Certificate is graduate-level so the PGWP is similar to a 2-year master's."**
Reality: PGWP duration is strictly tied to program length, not academic level. 1 year of study = 1 year of PGWP. Consequence: switching from 2-year master's to 1-year certificate loses 2 years of work permit and delays PR by 2–3 years.

**"If I'm Indian and in US tech, the green card is hard but doable — it just takes time."**
Reality: EB-2 India priority dates as of 2024 are around 2012. New filers face an estimated wait into the 2070s. Consequence: 10–15 years of career and life decisions made on a timeline that will never arrive within a working career.

**"A job offer boosts CRS by 50–200 points in Canada."**
Reality: As of March 25, 2025, job offer CRS points were eliminated. Consequence: strategies built around employer-based CRS gains produce no score increase; candidates miss ITAs they could have hit by investing in French or PNP instead.

**"University hospitals are cap-exempt for H-1B."**
Reality: Hospitals primarily providing medical care do not qualify even when university-affiliated. The "primary purpose" test is applied to the filing entity. Consequence: candidates accept positions expecting to bypass the lottery, then discover they're still subject to it with less OPT runway.

## User Psychology

Helps the AI recognize when a user's framing reveals a psychological trap and respond in a way that breaks through it rather than reinforcing it.

### Emotional States and What They Signal

| When user says... | Underlying state | Expert move |
|---|---|---|
| "I lost the H-1B lottery — what does this mean for me?" | Identity fusion with immigration status; self-worth tied to a random outcome | Explicitly decouple: "Your immigration status is a visa category decided by lottery, not a verdict on your value." Then redirect to concrete next-action mapping. |
| "I might have to leave anyway, so I'm not fully investing here" | Anticipatory grief; pre-paying the loss before it occurs | Name the logical flaw: "You're already paying the cost of leaving without leaving. The withdrawal isn't protecting you — it's costing you twice." |
| "I'll just consider Canada if H-1B fails again next year" (said 2+ years running) | Loss aversion; sunk cost in the US path | Quantify the waiting cost: "Every year you wait is X CRS points lost to age and Canadian work experience not accumulated. What is that worth?" |
| "Should I switch to a shorter program — the academic fit is better" | Present discomfort overweights distant PGWP cost | Make the cost near-term: "Switching costs you 2 years of PGWP. That's 2 years earlier you'd need to leave Canada or find another PR pathway." |
| "My CRS is 450 — is there any hope?" | Catastrophizing a static snapshot as permanent | Reframe as trajectory: "Run the calculator with French CLB 7 (+50), spouse language (+20), and one more year of work. What's the number in 18 months?" |
| "I have to be on my best behavior — I don't really belong here yet" | Immigration-induced imposter syndrome | "Your work authorization comes from IRCC/USCIS, not your employer's approval of your personality. What would you do here if you held PR?" |
| "Should I stay in the US or go to Canada?" (Indian/Chinese national) | Avoiding the core fear that the US GC may never come | Force the timeline math: "Look up today's EB-2 priority date for your country of birth. Calculate your age when it becomes current. Now compare to Canada PR in ~14 months." |
| "Researching Canada feels like giving up on the US" | Identity threat; Plan B research feels like self-betrayal | Decouple information from commitment: "Looking up your CRS score doesn't commit you to anything. You're adding data, not deciding." |
| "I'll just stop working since my EAD card hasn't arrived yet" | Authority deference to employer HR; catastrophizing | Surface the timely-filed rule: "If you have a receipt notice obtained before the EAD expired, you can continue working. Show HR the rule." |

### Reframing Moves

**"Should I stay in the US or go to Canada?"** → Given your country of birth, what is the realistic probability you'll receive a US green card within your working career — and does that probability justify the salary premium and immigration stress? Why it works: Shifts from a subjective country comparison to a concrete personal probability calculation that surfaces the per-country backlog as a primary variable.

**"My CRS is 450 and I'm stuck — what can I do?"** → Your CRS is not a fixed number — it's the sum of levers you haven't pulled yet. French bilingualism alone is worth 50 points and requires no employer or government cooperation. Why it works: Shifts from passive lottery-waiting to active lever-pulling; changes "when will I get lucky?" to "which 90-day actions will I take?"

**"Implied status means I can keep working while PGWP is pending"** → Implied status preserves your right to be in Canada. It does not preserve your right to work. These are two separate legal permissions. Why it works: Separates two concepts students conflate because their study permit bundled them, making the restriction concrete.

**"I lost the H-1B lottery — what does this say about me?"** → Your immigration status is not your self-worth. It's a visa category decided by lottery. Why it works: Removes shame and self-blame that make lottery loss feel catastrophic rather than logistical; restores capacity to problem-solve.

**"I'll consider Plan B if Plan A fails"** → A vague Plan B doesn't actually function as a backup. Research it concretely — visit, talk to people, model the salary and lifestyle — until you can genuinely say "I'd be okay with this." Only then does it reduce desperation. Why it works: A real Plan B reduces the psychological grip of single-path dependency, which improves decisions on the primary path too.

## How to Use This Skill

Always run Step 0 (Location Diagnosis) before giving any country-specific advice — generic advice that ignores country of birth, study permit submission date, or current status is worse than no advice. Apply the Universal Decision Framework first to establish whether the student is planning reactively, has contingencies, and is making decisions analytically rather than emotionally.
