## The Core Structural Difference: Canada vs. US vs. UK

Canadian PhD programs occupy a middle position in the global PhD landscape:
- More structure and coursework than UK PhDs (which are nearly pure research from day one)
- Less coursework-heavy than US programs (which require 2+ years of coursework before research)
- **Uniquely Canadian**: supervisor contact before applying is essentially mandatory, not optional

| Dimension | US PhD | Canadian PhD | UK PhD |
|-----------|--------|--------------|--------|
| Entry requirement | Usually bachelor's | Usually master's | Usually bachelor's or master's |
| Coursework | 2+ years required | 1-2 years (lighter) | Minimal to none |
| Time to completion | 5-6 years from BA | 4-5 years from MA | 3-4 years |
| Supervisor assignment | Year 2 typically | Before application | Before application |
| Pre-contact norm | Optional/helpful | Near-mandatory | Mandatory |
| Application process | Centralized + committee | Supervisor-first, then formal | Supervisor-first |
| Qualifying exams | Yes, formal | Yes, but varies | Minimal |
| GRE required | Usually yes | Usually NOT required | Not required |

## The Professor-First Application Culture

### Why it's different in Canada:

In the US: You apply to a program, a committee reviews, you rotate between labs in year 1, advisor assigned in year 2.

In Canada: You identify a potential supervisor FIRST, contact them, establish fit, and THEN apply formally. Many Canadian professors will not accept students who haven't contacted them beforehand. Some programs won't consider applications without a named supervisor willing to take the student.

> "A significant number of Canadian PhD admissions occur through supervisor-driven processes that don't align neatly with application calendars. This makes early professor contact critical." — Admit Lab

### The practical reason:
Canadian PhD funding is often supervisor-dependent. If a professor takes you on, they commit to funding you from their grant. Without that commitment, the department often won't admit you. The formal application is frequently a paper process ratifying a decision already made between student and supervisor.

## Pre-Application Timeline (What You Should Actually Do)

### August–September of the year before you want to start:
- Identify 4-8 target supervisors whose research aligns with yours
- Read their recent publications (last 2-3 years) thoroughly
- Check their lab websites for "prospective students" notes — many say "not taking students this cycle"
- Check their grant status: NSERC Award Search, CIHR funding database

### What your initial email to a Canadian professor should include:
1. Specific reference to 1-2 of their recent papers (not just "your research interests me")
2. Your research background and what you specifically can contribute to their lab
3. Clear articulation of why their research direction matches your questions
4. A concrete proposed research direction or question (not required to be final)
5. Attach: CV, transcripts, brief research statement (1 page max)
6. Subject line: "Prospective PhD Student — [Your Research Area]" (not "PhD Inquiry")

Length: 250-400 words. Professional, specific, not generic.

### If a professor responds positively:
- They will typically schedule a video call
- Treat this like an interview: prepare your research background, questions about the lab, knowledge of their work
- Ask about funding: "Do you have RA funding available for incoming students?" or "What is the typical funding structure for your PhD students?"
- Ask about current students: "Would it be possible to speak with a current PhD student in your lab?"

### September–October:
- Internal institutional funding deadlines (OGS at most Ontario schools; institutional scholarships)
- **These often come BEFORE the formal application deadline** — critical to know

### October:
- CGRS-D / federal award deadlines
- These are submitted through your institution or directly to the agency

### October–January:
- Formal program application submissions
- Most Canadian PhD programs have December–January deadlines

### March–April:
- Federal award decisions released
- Supervisor funding commitments confirmed

### April–June:
- Final offers extended
- Study permit (student visa) applications — must allow 3-4 months processing time

## Program Structure: What a Canadian PhD Actually Looks Like

### Years 1-2: Coursework and orientation
- Graduate seminars, research methods, directed studies
- Fewer required courses than US programs, but substantive
- Begin working in lab/supervisor's research group from day 1

### Mid-program: Qualifying/comprehensive exam
- Often Year 2 or 3
- Format varies: written exam + oral defense, or thesis proposal defense, or both
- "The comprehensive" is typically the formal transition to PhD candidacy

### Dissertation proposal
- Formal proposal to supervisor and committee
- Not expected to be final — refinement continues throughout

### Dissertation research and defense
- Independent research under supervisor
- Thesis committee provides oversight (typically 2-4 members plus supervisor)
- External examiner from another institution for defense

### Total timeline:
- Registration period: up to 6 years (some programs allow 7-8 with approval)
- Median actual completion (STEM): 5-6 years
- Median actual completion (humanities/social sciences): 6-8 years

## Key Mistakes International Students Make in Canadian PhD Applications

### Mistake 1: Applying without contacting supervisors
Many international students apply to Canadian PhD programs the same way they apply to US programs — filling out applications to programs without identifying a supervisor first. Canadian systems will often reject these outright or place applicants in limbo.

**Fix**: Contact 4-6 supervisors before applying to any program.

### Mistake 2: Missing internal funding deadlines
Many students focus on the program application deadline but miss the earlier internal funding deadline (OGS, institutional awards). These often close in October-November, months before the program application deadline of December-January.

**Fix**: On first contact with a department, ask: "What are the internal scholarship deadlines for incoming PhD students?"

### Mistake 3: Assuming funding is automatic
Unlike some US programs where PhD admission implies full funding, Canadian funding is NOT guaranteed. The formal offer letter may or may not include a specific funding commitment. Students have arrived in Canada without confirmed funding packages.

**Fix**: Get the funding commitment in writing before accepting.

### Mistake 4: Treating all Canadian schools the same
The "U15" (15 major research universities) range widely in funding culture, research output, and visa/PR prospects by location:
- UofT, UBC, McGill, Waterloo, Alberta: most international students; established immigration support
- Smaller institutions outside major cities: better Canadian experience for PR purposes (some PNPs favor non-Toronto/Vancouver)

### Mistake 5: Forgetting that GRE is usually not required
Most Canadian PhD programs do not require the GRE. Applicants who spend significant time and money on GRE prep for Canadian-only applications are misallocating effort.

## What "Funded" Means in Canada vs. US

**US funded PhD**: Program guarantees stipend + tuition waiver from admission. Funding is from the program; advisor is not the primary financial link.

**Canadian funded PhD**: Funding comes from combination of:
1. Supervisor's research grant (RA funding)
2. University fellowships and department scholarships
3. Teaching assistantships (TAships)
4. Competitive external awards (CGRS-D, OGS, Vanier)

This means: if your supervisor's grant runs out, your funding situation changes. The advisor-student relationship has a financial dimension that is more explicit in Canada than in the US. Asking about advisor funding status is not rude — it's responsible.

## Canadian PhD for International Students: Specific Considerations

### Study permit timing:
- Apply 3-4 months before program start
- Student permit tied to specific institution and program
- If you switch schools or programs, you may need a new permit

### Working during the PhD:
- On a valid study permit, you can work on-campus without a separate work permit
- TA and RA work at your institution is on-campus work = covered by study permit
- Off-campus work limited to 20 hours/week during academic sessions (some recent changes)

### Path to permanent residence after PhD:
See `phd_canada_immigration_path.txt` for full details.
- 3-year PGWP after PhD (as of February 2024 policy change)
- CEC eligibility after 1 year of skilled work
- CRS education points: 150 (single) or 140 (with spouse) for doctoral degree
- Ontario PhD Graduate Stream PNP available directly after graduation
