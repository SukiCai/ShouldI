## The Honest State of Canadian PhD Stipends (2024-2025)

### What the 2024 budget announced:
Canada's 2024 federal budget promised major increases to Tri-Council award values:
- PhD stipends (CGRS-D): raised to CAD $40,000/year (previously ~$24,000)
- Master's stipends (CGS-M): raised to CAD $27,000/year (previously $17,500)
- Postdoctoral fellowships: raised to CAD $70,000/year (previously ~$45,000)

### The critical caveat:
**Only Tri-Council (CGRS-D, Vanier, CGS-M) award holders receive these increases. The majority of PhD students funded through supervisor research grants are excluded.**

As Chemistry World reported (2024): "The vast majority of students and postdocs will continue to struggle financially."

A 2023 national survey found nearly **90% of Canadian graduate students reported financial stress**, with **almost one-third considering leaving academia due to money pressures**.

## University-by-University Stipend Overview

### University of British Columbia (UBC)
**Official minimum funding policy:**
- September 2018: $18,000/year minimum
- September 2021: $22,000/year minimum
- September 2024: $24,000/year minimum
- September 2026: $40,000/year minimum (announced)

**Current reality**: PhD students at UBC-Vancouver receive an average of $41,000/year (as of 2024 data, driven by above-average funding in STEM programs and growing supervisor grant sizes)

**Applies to**: All full-time PhD students at UBC-Vancouver who started September 2018 or later. Excludes part-time, EdD, DMA.

### University of Toronto
- Students entering 2023 or later in most funded PhD programs: tuition + incidentals + ~$21,000 living allowance = approximately $37,000 total package
- MD/PhD program specific: similar total
- SGS publishes a PhD Funding Dashboard (interactive, accessible on SGS website)
- Significant variation by department: engineering/CS typically higher than humanities

### McGill University
- 95% of PhD students have full tuition covered
- International PhD students receive funding to cover differential between Quebec resident and international tuition
- Chemistry PhD department: fixed stipend approximately CAD $26,000/year (reported)
- Total package varies significantly by supervisor and department

### University of Waterloo
- Graduated increase in minimum funding in recent years
- CS/Engineering: typically competitive stipends; top supervisors often pay above minimum
- Specific 2024-2025 minimums: consult individual department

### University of Alberta
- CGRS-D provides $40,000/year for winners
- Base institutional funding varies by department
- Alberta Innovates Graduate Scholarships: additional $22,500/year for eligible research

## Field-by-Field Stipend Realities

### STEM (Computer Science, Engineering, Physics, Chemistry, Biology):
- Range: $22,000–$45,000+ CAD/year
- Top CS/ML labs: $30,000–$45,000 (especially at Waterloo, UofT, UBC, Mila-affiliated programs)
- Experimental sciences: $22,000–$35,000 typical; variable with supervisor grant size
- NSF GRFP equivalent (CGRS-D): $40,000 for winners, competitive
- TA supplement common: $3,000–$8,000/year for 1-2 TAships

### Social Sciences and Humanities:
- Range: $17,000–$28,000 CAD/year (significantly lower than STEM)
- SSHRC CGRS-D: $40,000/year for winners, but fewer awards per field
- Many humanities PhDs survive on OGS ($15,000) + TAships + minimal supervisor funding
- Risk: humanities PhDs frequently face funding gaps in years 5+

### Health Sciences (Medicine, Nursing, Public Health):
- Range: $22,000–$40,000 CAD/year
- CIHR CGRS-D for winners: $40,000
- Hospital-affiliated labs often have supplemental funding
- MD/PhD programs: often more generous packages ($35,000–$45,000)

## CAD vs. USD: The Comparison Every International Student Needs

As of 2024, CAD/USD exchange rate is approximately 0.73-0.75 (1 CAD ≈ 0.73-0.75 USD).

| Canadian Stipend (CAD) | US Equivalent (USD) | Context |
|------------------------|---------------------|---------|
| $24,000 CAD | ~$17,500 USD | Below US minimum at most R1 universities |
| $30,000 CAD | ~$22,000 USD | Roughly comparable to low-end US stipends |
| $40,000 CAD | ~$29,000 USD | Comparable to mid-range US R1 stipends |
| $50,000 CAD | ~$36,500 USD | Competitive with top US programs |

**BUT**: Canada has universal healthcare. Students on study permits pay into provincial health insurance at low cost (Ontario UHIP: ~$756/year; BC MSP: subsidized). In US, PhD students pay full medical premiums (~$2,000–$6,000/year out of pocket or covered by university). Adjust comparison accordingly.

**Adjusted comparison**: A $30,000 CAD Canadian stipend with universal healthcare is roughly equivalent in real purchasing power to a $22,000–$24,000 USD US stipend with employer-covered health insurance.

## Cost of Living Context

Major Canadian PhD cities (2024 approximate):
- **Toronto (UofT)**: $2,200–$3,200/month for 1BR. Stipend tension is significant; $22,000/year = $1,833/month pre-tax, which covers rent but leaves little margin.
- **Vancouver (UBC)**: $2,100–$3,000/month for 1BR. Similar pressure to Toronto.
- **Waterloo**: $1,400–$1,900/month for 1BR. More manageable on typical stipends.
- **Montreal (McGill)**: $1,300–$1,800/month for 1BR. Best cost-of-living among major research cities. French-language programs often have lower costs.
- **Edmonton (UAlberta)**: $1,100–$1,600/month for 1BR. Most affordable major city.

**Practical threshold**: For comfortable PhD life in Canada (not just survival), students need approximately CAD $28,000–$32,000/year in major cities. The announced $40,000 minimum for CGRS-D holders will reach this threshold; base institutional funding in many programs still falls short.

## Key Questions to Ask Specifically About Canadian Stipends

When receiving a PhD offer from a Canadian program, ask:

1. **"What is the exact total annual funding package, broken down by source?"**
   (Supervisor RA + TAship + institutional fellowship + external award)

2. **"Is any portion of this contingent on winning external competitions?"**
   (If yes: what is the guaranteed baseline if you don't win OGS/CGRS-D?)

3. **"What is the funding duration — all 4-5 years, or just the first 2?"**
   (Some offers guarantee funding only for the first 2 years)

4. **"What happens to my funding if my supervisor's grant runs out?"**
   (Rarely asked; critical to know; a good supervisor will have a clear answer)

5. **"What is the TAship requirement? How many hours per week typically?"**
   (Excess TAship demands are a common hidden cost to research time)

6. **"What do current 3rd-5th year students in your group earn annually?"**
   (Most reliable measure of actual funding, not promises)
