## The Uncomfortable Truth About Unfunded Master's Degrees

**43% of master's degree programs yield negative financial returns.**

Source: Foundation for Research on Equal Opportunity, analysis of 53,000+ degrees using Department of Education and Census Bureau data, reported by Fortune (November 28, 2024). The study found that nearly double the proportion of master's programs (43%) have negative ROI compared to bachelor's programs (23%).

This is the single most important fact for any international student considering a self-funded MS degree.

**Comparison anchor**: Bachelor's degree median ROI is approximately $160,000 lifetime — higher than most master's degrees, because bachelor's programs cost less and take less time.

## The International Student Multiplier Problem

For international students on F-1 visas, the ROI calculation is further distorted by employment risk:

### Employment gap after graduation:
- **44.6%** of international graduates employed after graduation
- **62.1%** of domestic students employed after graduation
- International students apply to **2x as many jobs** but receive **30% fewer offers**
- Primary cause: sponsorship requirement, not skill gap

Source: Extern.com US Job Market International Students Guide (2026)

### This means:
An international CS MS student facing a 44.6% employment rate has a ~55% chance of NOT finding a job in the standard OPT window — even in the best-ROI field.

## OPT Pipeline Reality: The Timeline That Determines Everything

### Standard OPT (all international MS graduates):
- **Duration**: 12 months post-graduation
- **Work authorization**: Any job in your field; employer doesn't need to sponsor
- **Unemployment limit**: 90 aggregate days during OPT; exceeding this jeopardizes F-1 status

### STEM OPT Extension (STEM degrees only):
- **Additional**: 24 months on top of standard OPT = 36 months total
- **Requirement**: Employer must be enrolled in E-Verify
- **Purpose**: Provides 3 H-1B lottery attempts
- **Competition growing**: 95,384 students on STEM OPT extension in 2025, a 54% year-over-year increase

### What determines if you get STEM OPT:
Not just your field — your CIP code. The program's CIP (Classification of Instructional Programs) code must appear on the official STEM OPT Designated Degree Program List maintained by DHS. A "Data Analytics" degree may or may not qualify depending on the CIP code. A "Computer Science" degree almost always qualifies. Verify the CIP code with the DSO before enrolling.

**Critical**: This is why the school and program name matter less than the CIP code for international students targeting long-term US employment.

## H-1B Lottery Realities After OPT

OPT is a bridge, not a destination. The bridge leads to H-1B (or departure).

### H-1B FY2026 selection rates (new wage-based lottery system):
- Entry-level positions: ~15% selection rate
- Senior roles: ~61% selection rate
- Overall FY2026: 35.3% (336,153 registrations for 85,000 spots)

Source: Extern.com (2026)

### Implications for MS graduates:
A new MS graduate is typically entry-level → ~15% H-1B selection rate per year → with STEM OPT (3 attempts): cumulative probability of at least one selection ≈ 1 - (0.85)³ ≈ 39%.

**This means roughly 6 out of 10 international MS students with STEM OPT will NOT get an H-1B despite 3 attempts.**

### Top H-1B sponsors (relevant for job targeting):
- Amazon (~12,400 approvals), TCS (5,500), Microsoft (5,200), Apple (4,200), Google (4,200) — these companies actively sponsor and can absorb the cost ($2,200–$6,400+ per petition plus attorney fees)
- Small and mid-size companies often cannot or will not sponsor

**Job search strategy implication**: For international MS students, targeting large tech employers who are known H-1B sponsors is not optional — it is a survival strategy.

## The Break-Even Calculation for Unfunded MS

### Inputs:
- Total MS cost: tuition + living + opportunity cost
- Salary premium: salary-with-MS minus salary-without-MS
- Years employed on STEM OPT / H-1B before needing PR or departure

### Example: CS MS at a mid-tier US program
- Total cost: $80,000 (tuition) + $40,000 (living 2 years) + $80,000 (foregone salary) = $200,000 real cost
- Salary premium with CS MS vs. without (for jobs international student could get): ~$20,000–$30,000/year
- Break-even: 7–10 years of employment
- Probability of 7 years continuous US employment (STEM OPT + H-1B + green card): uncertain; depends heavily on country of birth (Indian/Chinese nationals face EB backlog of 50+ years)

### Example: Education MS at a mid-tier US program
- Total cost: $60,000 (tuition) + $30,000 (living) = $90,000
- Salary premium: ~$5,000–$8,000/year
- Break-even: 12–18 years
- Immigration risk: education is rarely H-1B sponsored; STEM OPT not available
- Assessment: Financially negative for most international students

## The Questions to Ask Before Enrolling in an Unfunded MS

An expert advisor would ask a prospective MS student:

1. **"What is the school's STEM OPT CIP code, and is it on the DHS designated list?"**
   (Critical for international students in any field that might qualify)

2. **"What percentage of graduates in this program found employment within 6 months of graduation?"**
   (Not the school's overall rate — this specific program's rate)

3. **"Which employers recruited from this program in the last 2 years? Do they sponsor H-1B?"**
   (Proof-of-pipeline question — careers page listings are marketing, not evidence)

4. **"What is the total cost including living expenses, and what is my break-even timeline given realistic US employment prospects?"**
   (Most students calculate tuition only, ignoring opportunity cost and employment risk)

5. **"If I do not get H-1B after 3 attempts, what is my plan?"**
   (Canadian graduate PR path, return home, third country — this should be a real plan, not "I'll figure it out")

## When Unfunded MS Is Still Worth It (Despite the Risks)

An unfunded MS can still be the right decision when:

- Target field is CS/ML/Data Science or Engineering (positive expected ROI even accounting for H-1B risk)
- Student is from a country with short EB green card queues (not India or China)
- Student has a strong existing employer relationship who will sponsor
- Student has a clear Plan B that doesn't depend on H-1B (Canadian PhD → PR, return home, etc.)
- MS is specifically needed to change fields and the field change dramatically increases earning potential

An unfunded MS is almost certainly the wrong decision when:
- Field has historically negative MS ROI (education, arts, social sciences, humanities)
- Student is from India or China (EB-2/EB-3 backlog eliminates long-term US path regardless)
- The motivation is "I couldn't get into a PhD program" without addressing the root cause
- The student cannot afford the tuition without loans and has no employment probability analysis
