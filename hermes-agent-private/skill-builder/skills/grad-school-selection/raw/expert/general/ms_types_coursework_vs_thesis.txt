## Two Fundamentally Different Degrees With the Same Name

"Master's degree" covers two structurally different programs that most applicants treat as interchangeable. They are not.

---

## Coursework-Only MS (MCS, MEng, Professional MS)

**What it is:**
- 30 credit hours of graduate coursework, no research requirement
- Duration: 1 year full-time; 2-3 years part-time
- No thesis; may have a capstone project or comprehensive exam
- Self-funded (tuition typically $30,000–$80,000 total for international students)
- Common degree names: MCS (Master of Computer Science), MEng, MEM, MPH (professional), MBA

**What it trains you for:**
- Breadth across the field (sampling many subjects vs. depth in one)
- Immediate industry integration: software engineer, data engineer, product manager, cybersecurity roles
- Credential signaling: demonstrates graduate-level knowledge to employers

**Median salary outcomes (CS-specific, Rice University data):**
- MCS graduates → software/data/product roles → median salary approximately $126,830

**Who should choose this:**
- Career changers or breadth-seekers who know they want industry roles
- Students who need the credential for a specific job market signal
- Professionals doing part-time degrees while employed

**Who should NOT choose this for PhD goals:**
- Anyone planning to apply to PhD programs without SEPARATE research experience — a coursework MS with no research component gives PhD programs nothing to evaluate your research ability on
- Exception: if you secure an RA/research position at your institution independently alongside the coursework degree

---

## Thesis MS (MSCS, MS with thesis, MSc)

**What it is:**
- 30-40 credit hours including a thesis or significant research component
- Duration: 1.5–2 years full-time
- Requires: advisor relationship, original research, written thesis, oral defense before committee
- May include partial TA/RA funding (varies widely — less common than PhD funding)
- Common degree names: MSCS, MSc, MS (thesis track)

**What it trains you for:**
- Research methodology: how to identify a problem, conduct original inquiry, and defend conclusions
- Depth in a specific subfield (narrower than coursework MS)
- Collaboration with a research advisor and committee

**Median salary outcomes (CS-specific):**
- Research Scientist / R&D roles → median salary approximately $131,490

**Who should choose this:**
- Students planning to apply to PhD programs — provides the research evidence PhD applications require
- Students interested in R&D, innovation labs, or industry research scientist roles
- Students who want to test PhD-style work before committing to a full PhD

---

## Side-by-Side Comparison

| Dimension | Coursework MS | Thesis MS |
|-----------|---------------|-----------|
| Duration | 1 year FT | 1.5–2 years FT |
| Research component | None / capstone only | Original research + defense |
| Advisor relationship | None | Required (thesis advisor) |
| PhD stepping stone? | Only if + separate RA work | Yes, directly |
| Funding availability | Rarely | Occasionally (TA/RA) |
| Tuition cost | Full cost always | Sometimes partial waiver with RA |
| Industry career track | Strong | Strong, but more specialized |
| GRE typically required | Less often | More often |
| Breadth vs. depth | Breadth (many courses) | Depth (one research area) |

## The "Project Option" — Middle Ground

Some programs offer a third track: a "Master's project" or "master's by project," which:
- Requires a substantial original project (not necessarily academic research)
- Has a supervisor but less formal than a thesis
- Takes 1–1.5 years
- Does NOT typically substitute for thesis MS in PhD applications (PhD programs may or may not accept it as research evidence)

If your goal is PhD preparation, ask the program explicitly: "Will this project count as research experience for PhD applications?"

---

## Common Misunderstanding: "I'll do a coursework MS then apply to PhD"

This works only if:
1. You secure a formal RA position or publish research during the MS
2. OR the PhD program offers direct-entry pathways where your undergraduate research is sufficient

Without research output during the coursework MS, PhD applications will lack the strongest evidence of research potential. Many applicants are surprised when their "MS degree" doesn't automatically strengthen their PhD application.

**Fix**: If you've already started a coursework MS and want to apply to PhD programs, proactively approach faculty for research opportunities. Even one RA position with a first-author abstract or co-authored paper changes the application significantly.
