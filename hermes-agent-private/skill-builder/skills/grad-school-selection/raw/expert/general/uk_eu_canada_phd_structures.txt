## Why Non-US PhD Structures Matter for Decision-Making

Most PhD advice is written from a US perspective: 5–7 year programs, coursework + qualifying exams + dissertation, RA/TA funding from advisors or departments. Students who consider non-US programs often apply without understanding how fundamentally the structure differs — and those differences have major consequences for immigration, career, and financial planning.

This section covers UK, EU, and Canada PhD programs for students choosing between them and US programs.

---

## United Kingdom

### Program Structure

UK PhDs are research-only from day one. There is typically:
- No coursework requirement (or minimal structured training in year 1)
- No qualifying examinations
- A thesis submitted and defended after 3–4 years (funded duration: typically 3 years, sometimes 3.5–4)
- A viva voce (oral defense) with 2 examiners — typically 1 internal, 1 external

The absence of coursework means you need a clear research project before you start. You are not admitted to explore a field — you are admitted to execute a project.

### Funding Sources

UK PhDs for international students are expensive without funding:
- Tuition: £15,000–£35,000/year for international students
- Stipend (if funded): approximately £19,000–£22,000/year (UKRI 2024 rate)

Funded routes for international students:
- **UKRI (UK Research and Innovation)** studentships: most common, funded by research councils (AHRC, BBSRC, EPSRC, ESRC, MRC, NERC). Some studentships are international-eligible; many are UK/EU only — check carefully.
- **University scholarships**: competitive internal funding, increasingly available to international students
- **Commonwealth Scholarships**: for citizens of Commonwealth nations
- **Chevening Scholarships**: UK government scholarship for master's (not PhD) — sometimes leads to self-funded PhD
- **Industrial CASE studentships**: co-funded by industry partners, often more available to international students

Warning: Many UK PhD advertisements describe "funding available" but mean funding only for UK/EU applicants. Verify eligibility explicitly.

### Immigration (UK)

- **Student visa** (formerly Tier 4): required for international PhD students
- Duration: typically granted for program length + 1 year
- Work rights: up to 20 hours/week during term; full-time during holidays
- **Graduate Route visa**: after completing your PhD (or any eligible degree), you receive 2 years of open work authorization (3 years for PhD holders). This is the UK's equivalent of OPT.
- No points-based immigration pathway directly from Graduate Route, but 2-3 years of work can build toward Skilled Worker visa

Key difference from US: UK Graduate Route is automatic on program completion, no lottery, open market. Similar to STEM OPT but without employer e-verify requirement.

### Advisor Relationship

In the UK, your "supervisor" (not "advisor") is typically your primary point of contact. Co-supervision (2 supervisors) is increasingly common. The relationship is more directive than US models — your supervisor often defines the research project, particularly in STEM.

Questions to ask UK supervisors:
- "Is the project fixed, or is there scope to develop my own direction within it?"
- "How often do you typically meet with your PhD students?"
- "Have any of your recent students graduated within the funded period (3–3.5 years)?"

## European Union

### Program Structure

EU PhD programs vary significantly by country, but common features include:
- **Shorter duration**: 3–4 years is standard (vs. 5–7 US)
- **Research-only**: minimal or no coursework
- **Salary, not stipend**: many EU PhD positions are employment contracts with salary, benefits, and social security contributions — not scholarships
- **Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions (MSCA)**: EU-funded program providing ~€38,000–€50,000/year (gross) for 3-year doctoral positions, with international mobility requirement

Notable differences by country:
- **Germany**: PhD is typically an employment contract (Wissenschaftlicher Mitarbeiter); strong research culture; no language requirement in many programs (English-medium programs available); can lead to Niederlassungserlaubnis (settlement permit) after time in country
- **Netherlands**: structured 4-year programs (AIO positions); funded as employment; competitive
- **Sweden**: funded employment; strong work-life balance norms; clear 4-year completion expectation
- **France**: more variable; CIFRE doctoral contracts available (industry co-funded); less well-funded than Germany/Netherlands without competitive fellowship

### MSCA Doctoral Networks

For 2024-2028 cycle, MSCA DN positions:
- Open to any nationality
- Require mobility (you must not have resided in the country for 12+ months in the past 3 years at time of appointment)
- Provide approximately €3,500–€4,200/month gross + mobility allowance
- Include mandatory secondments at partner institutions

MSCA is one of the few PhD funding mechanisms truly open to international students with no citizenship restriction.

### Immigration (EU)

Each EU country has its own immigration rules, but general patterns:
- Germany: residence permit for PhD study; after PhD, 18-month job search permit; long-term prospects for PR via employment
- Netherlands: residence permit for study; orientation year permit after graduation (12 months to find work)
- For international students interested in Europe as a long-term destination: EU PhD + employment can lead to permanent residence in 5 years in many countries

### Career Considerations

EU/UK PhDs are 3–4 years, which means:
- Shorter time to degree = faster career progression
- Less TA experience = potentially weaker teaching portfolio for academic jobs
- Often more publication-focused earlier in program
- The compressed timeline means the first 1-2 years must be research-productive almost immediately

For academic careers, European PhD holders face additional job market friction when competing for US academic positions (US programs prioritize their own graduates for early-career placements). This is less of an issue for industry roles.

## Canada

### Program Structure

Canadian PhD programs are structurally most similar to US programs:
- 4–6 years typical (though 4–5 is expected in many programs)
- Coursework in years 1–2 + qualifying exams + dissertation
- RA/TA funding model similar to US
- Stipend range: CAD $18,000–$35,000/year (varies significantly by field and university)

Key differences from US:
- **SSHRC, NSERC, CIHR**: Canada's tri-council research funding agencies. Graduate fellowships (SSHRC Doctoral, NSERC CGS-D, CIHR Fellowship) are prestigious and portable — you bring them to any eligible Canadian institution
- Stipends are generally lower than US in absolute terms but lower cost of living in many cities (though not Toronto/Vancouver)
- French-language programs at Quebec universities have different application processes and may have lower international tuition (with Quebec-specific agreements)

### Immigration — The Critical Advantage

Canada offers the clearest pathway from PhD to permanent residence of any English-speaking country:

1. **Post-Graduation Work Permit (PGWP)**: Open work permit issued after completing a PhD (or any degree program). Duration = length of program, up to 3 years. No employer requirement, no lottery, no cap.

2. **Express Entry + Canadian Experience Class (CEC)**: After 1 year of skilled work in Canada on PGWP, you are eligible for CEC. With a PhD degree and Canadian work experience, CRS scores are strong. Many PhD graduates can receive ITA (Invitation to Apply for PR) within 6–12 months of PGWP employment.

3. **Provincial Nominee Programs (PNPs)**: Many provinces have streams specifically for international graduates, sometimes with lower CRS score requirements.

**Realistic timeline**: PhD completion → PGWP → 1 year work → CEC draw → PR application → PR = approximately 2–3 years after PhD completion.

This pathway is substantially clearer than the US route (H-1B lottery + green card backlog for many nationalities).

### Comparison: Canadian PhD vs. US PhD for International Students

| Dimension | US PhD | Canadian PhD |
|-----------|--------|--------------|
| Stipend | USD $30K–45K | CAD $18K–35K |
| PR pathway | H-1B lottery → long wait | PGWP → CEC → Express Entry (2-3 years) |
| Time to PR | 10-20+ years (varies by nationality) | 3-5 years post-PhD |
| Program length | 5-7 years | 4-6 years |
| Research funding | More abundant (NSF, NIH) | Growing (NSERC, SSHRC, CIHR) |
| Academic job market | More competitive, more positions | Smaller market, fewer positions |
| Major cities | Multiple tech/research hubs | Toronto, Vancouver, Montreal |

For students from India and China (where US green card backlogs are multi-decade), a Canadian PhD + PR pathway is often dramatically more efficient for long-term settlement goals.

### Practical Questions for Canadian PhD Programs

- "What is the typical stipend, and is it 12-month or 9-month?"
- "Do students apply for NSERC/SSHRC in their first year? What is the department's success rate?"
- "Is the PGWP application process well-supported by your international student office?"
- "What percentage of your international PhD graduates apply for PR through Express Entry?"
