## Ontario Graduate Scholarship (OGS)

### What it is:
Merit-based provincial scholarship for graduate students at Ontario universities. Funded jointly by the Ontario government and participating universities (government pays 2/3, institution pays 1/3).

### Amount:
- **$5,000 per session** (one academic term ≈ 4 months)
- **$10,000 for two consecutive sessions** (most master's recipients)
- **$15,000 for three consecutive sessions** (most PhD recipients)

Note: Applicants can receive 2 or 3 sessions in a row; the OGS is typically held for 12 months across 3 terms or 8 months across 2 terms.

### Eligibility:
**Academic requirements:**
- Minimum A− (80%) average over the preceding two full-time years of study
- Full-time enrollment required

**Citizenship:**
- Canadian citizens and permanent residents
- **International students on study permits are also eligible** — institutions receive a set allocation specifically for international students (varies by institution; typically 15-30% of total OGS allocations)

**Limits:**
- Maximum 2 OGS awards at master's level (before end of 2nd year)
- Maximum 4 OGS awards at doctoral level (before end of 5th year)
- Cannot be combined with other Ontario government student assistance in the same period

### Application process:
- Apply through your institution's graduate studies office
- Application period: typically October–November for the following academic year
- Each institution has their own internal deadline and selection process
- Results typically released February–April
- Institutions set their own additional criteria within provincial requirements

### Who is competitive:
- A− or higher average (80%+)
- Strong research record (publications, conference presentations, awards)
- Compelling research statement
- Competitive among students within the same program

### Stacking with Tri-Council awards:
OGS CAN be held concurrently with some other awards, but not all. Check with your institution:
- CGRS-D holders: often still eligible for OGS top-up depending on institutional policy
- Vanier holders: typically receive institution top-up instead of OGS

## QEII Graduate Scholarship in Science and Technology (QEII-GSST)

Available at Ontario universities; co-funded by provincial government and private sector donors.
- Similar structure to OGS ($5,000/session, up to $15,000/year)
- Specifically for STEM fields
- Students cannot hold OGS and QEII-GSST simultaneously — choose one
- Some institutions offer one or the other; check availability at your target school

## Other Provincial Graduate Award Programs

### British Columbia
**BC Graduate Scholarship**: Administered through individual institutions from provincial funding. Amounts and competition vary by institution.

**BC PNP (Provincial Nominee Program) — International Post-Graduate Category**: PhD and master's graduates from BC can apply for provincial nomination toward permanent residence with no job offer required. Particularly useful post-graduation.

### Quebec
**FRQSC (Fonds de Recherche du Québec – Société et Culture)**: Social sciences and humanities doctoral scholarships, typically CAD $25,000-$35,000/year for Quebec residents and students at Quebec institutions.

**FRQNT (Fonds de Recherche du Québec – Nature et Technologies)**: Natural sciences and engineering equivalent, similar amounts.

**Note**: Quebec scholarships are primarily for Quebec residents or students at Quebec institutions. International students may be eligible depending on program. French language ability is an asset but not always required for English-stream programs.

### Alberta
**Alberta Graduate Excellence Scholarship**: Merit-based; administered through institutions. Amounts vary widely.

**Alberta Innovates Graduate Student Scholarships**: For STEM graduate students with applied/industry-relevant research. Up to CAD $22,500/year.

### Nova Scotia / Atlantic Provinces
Smaller award ecosystem; less provincial funding than Ontario/BC/Quebec. Federal awards become relatively more important.

## Strategic Comparison: OGS vs. CGRS-D vs. University Funding

| Award | Amount | Who can get it | Administered by |
|-------|--------|----------------|-----------------|
| CGRS-D | $40,000/yr | Competitive applicants; Canadian + some int'l | Federal (NSERC/SSHRC/CIHR) |
| OGS | $15,000/yr | Stronger pool of Ontario students | Province + institution |
| QEII-GSST | $15,000/yr | STEM students at Ontario universities | Province + private sector |
| Vanier | $50,000/yr | Top 1% nationally | Federal, institution-nominated |
| University base package | $18,000–$30,000/yr | All funded PhD admits | Institution |

**Practical stacking example** at a well-funded Ontario university:
- University base stipend: $22,000/year
- OGS (if won): +$15,000/year
- CGRS-D (if won): replaces or supplements to $40,000 total
- Supervisor top-up: varies by advisor

Total range for a competitive PhD student at UofT/McMaster/Western/Queens:
- Without awards: $22,000–$26,000/year
- With OGS: $30,000–$37,000/year
- With CGRS-D: $40,000–$45,000/year (institutional top-up)
- With Vanier: $50,000–$55,000/year (institutional top-up)

## Key Questions to Ask During PhD Admission

1. "What is the base funding package for incoming PhD students in this program?"
2. "What percentage of your PhD students receive OGS or CGRS-D?"
3. "Does the department/supervisor top up federal or provincial awards?"
4. "Is there an international student allocation for OGS at this institution?"
5. "What is the typical total annual compensation for a 3rd-year PhD student?"

These questions reveal whether the funding culture is transparent, generous, or opaque — which often correlates with advisor and program quality.
