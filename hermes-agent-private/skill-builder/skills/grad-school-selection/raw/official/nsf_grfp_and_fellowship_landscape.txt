## NSF Graduate Research Fellowship Program (GRFP)

### What It Is
The NSF GRFP is the most prestigious and broadly applicable US graduate fellowship for STEM PhD students. It provides:
- $37,000/year stipend (FY2024 rate)
- $16,000/year cost-of-education allowance paid to the institution
- 3 years of support over a 5-year tenure period
- Portable: can be used at any accredited US institution

### Eligibility
- US citizen, national, or permanent resident (NOT open to international students on F-1/J-1)
- Early career: must apply in first or second year of graduate school, OR in the final year of undergraduate study
- Any STEM field (including social sciences, psychology, economics when using quantitative/scientific methods)
- Cannot have previously received a federal graduate fellowship or been enrolled in a PhD program for more than 12 months at time of application

### Strategic implications for applicants
1. Applying before PhD enrollment (as a senior undergrad) is allowed and produces stronger applications in some fields — no GPR restriction exists at that stage
2. NSF Fellows can negotiate with programs: a GRFP winner brings their own $37K stipend and $16K to the institution, giving you leverage to negotiate on other dimensions (conference travel, summer funding, advisor match)
3. Not all departments count GRFP stipend toward their standard package — ask explicitly whether GRFP is additive or substitutive for department funding
4. GRFP applications require two essays: Personal Statement (3 pages) and Research Proposal (2 pages). Broad impact and intellectual merit are both evaluated.

### GRFP and international students
International students are NOT eligible for NSF GRFP. This is a significant funding asymmetry between domestic and international PhD students that affects negotiating position and the value of specific fellowships.

## NIH Ruth L. Kirschstein National Research Service Awards (NRSA)

### What It Is
NIH's primary mechanism for predoctoral fellowship support in biomedical and behavioral sciences:
- F31: Individual National Research Service Award (predoctoral)
- Stipend: approximately $24,000/year (FY2024)
- Duration: up to 5 years
- Requires institutional sponsorship (your PI and department)

### Eligibility
- US citizen, national, or permanent resident (NOT open to international students)
- Pursuing a research doctoral degree
- Must have a mentor/sponsor at an NIH-eligible institution

### Strategic use
F31 success strengthens your graduate record and signals independent research capability. Principal investigators value F31 because it comes with indirect cost support to the institution and demonstrates the student's independent fundability.

## Department of Defense (DoD) Fellowships

Several DoD-funded fellowship programs support STEM PhD students:

**NDSEG (National Defense Science and Engineering Graduate Fellowship)**
- $38,400/year stipend (FY2024)
- Full tuition + fees
- US citizens or nationals only (NOT permanent residents, NOT international students)
- Fields: aeronautical/astronautical engineering, biosciences, chemical engineering, chemistry, cognitive neuroscience, computer science, electrical engineering, geosciences, materials science, mathematics, mechanical engineering, naval architecture/ocean engineering, oceanography, physics
- Duration: 3 years

**SMART Scholarship (Science, Mathematics, and Research for Transformation)**
- DoD employment commitment after graduation
- Full tuition + stipend ($25,000–$38,000/year depending on degree level)
- Requires summer internship and post-graduation service commitment
- US citizens only

## Ford Foundation Predoctoral Fellowship

- Focus: diversity in higher education — supports students from underrepresented groups
- $27,000/year stipend for 3 years
- US citizens, nationals, or permanent residents studying at US institutions
- Open to doctoral students in research-based fields (not professional degrees)
- Strong in social sciences and humanities as well as STEM

## External Fellowships Relevant to International Students

Most major US federal fellowships exclude international students. International PhD students' primary fellowship options:

**Institutional fellowships**: awarded by the department or university (often competitive internal application)

**Private foundations**: some support international students
- Hertz Foundation: US citizens/nationals only
- Paul & Daisy Soros Fellowships: for New Americans (immigrants or children of immigrants) — includes eligible international students who are permanent residents
- Gates Cambridge: for admission to Cambridge, not US institutions

**Home country funding**: many governments fund PhD study abroad (China Scholarship Council, Korea Research Foundation, etc.)

**Field-specific organizations**: professional societies in many fields offer fellowships without citizenship restrictions

**The practical implication**: International PhD students should negotiate harder on institutional fellowship packages because they cannot access the federal fellowship pool that domestic students can. A department that offers comparable packages to domestic and international students is effectively underfunding international students who cannot supplement with NSF GRFP or NRSA.

## Fellowship Strategy for PhD Applicants

### For domestic (US citizen/PR) STEM applicants:
1. Apply for NSF GRFP in senior year of undergrad (first shot before the "12-month enrolled" restriction)
2. If not awarded, apply in first year of PhD
3. NSF fellowship materially improves your negotiating position with programs
4. Check whether your target programs have block grants (NSF, NIH, DoD training grants) that provide guaranteed fellowship support to incoming students

### For international students:
1. Ask each program explicitly: "What fellowship support is available to international students?"
2. Ask whether the department has institutional fellowships (not grants) that are not tied to citizenship
3. Ask whether you would be funded through RA (from advisor grants) or TA — and understand the risk profile of each
4. Consider applying to NSF GRFP for domestic students who are your peers: if they win it and bring funding to the lab, it may free up grant money that benefits the whole lab including you

### For humanities applicants:
1. ACLS (American Council of Learned Societies): the most prestigious humanities post-doctoral and advanced doctoral fellowship; ask faculty what role it plays in your department
2. Mellon Foundation: significant funder of humanities fellowships, often through institutions
3. Woodrow Wilson National Fellowship Foundation (select fields)
4. FLAS (Foreign Language and Area Studies): if your work involves a foreign language, FLAS provides $18,000/year stipends with language study support — open to US citizens/PRs

## STEM Designated Degree Program List (for OPT/STEM OPT)

For international students, the CIP code of the degree determines OPT eligibility:
- Standard OPT: 12 months for any degree program
- STEM OPT extension: additional 24 months ONLY for degrees on the STEM Designated Degree Program List

The STEM OPT extension is available for designated STEM fields (e.g., Computer Science CIP 11.07xx, Electrical Engineering CIP 14.10xx) but NOT for adjacent non-STEM fields (e.g., Information Systems CIP 52.12xx, which is a business program).

Applicants should verify their specific program's CIP code with the DSO before accepting, not after — the difference between 12 months and 36 months of OPT is enormous for immigration planning.

The official STEM Designated Degree Program List is maintained by ICE/SEVP and is updated periodically. The program name matters less than the CIP code.
