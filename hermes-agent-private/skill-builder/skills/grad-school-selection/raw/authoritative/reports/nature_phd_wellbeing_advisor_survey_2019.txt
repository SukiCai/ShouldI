## Survey Overview

**Scope**: 6,300+ PhD students worldwide, Nature's fifth biennial survey
**Conducted by**: Nature journal in partnership with Shift Learning (UK education market research)
**Year**: 2019
**Coverage**: Mental health, advisor relationships, bullying, career concerns, work hours, finances

---

## Mental Health Data

- **36%** sought help for anxiety or depression caused by their PhD studies
- A 2018 comparison study found **39%** of PhD students scored in the moderate-to-severe depression range, compared to **6%** of the general population — a 6x higher rate
- **18%** did not feel supported at their institution
- **9%** wanted mental health help but found none available on their campus
- Of the 36% who sought help, **43%** sought it through their institution
- Of those who used institutional support, only **26%** found it helpful

**Interpretation**: Formal institutional mental health resources are available to most PhD students but are widely considered inadequate. 2 out of 3 students who use institutional support don't find it helpful.

---

## Advisor Relationship Data (Critical for Decision-Making)

- **50%** received less than one hour per week of one-on-one time with their advisor
- **35%** received one to three hours weekly
- **12%** received more than three hours weekly

### The most actionable statistic in the survey:
**Nearly 25% would change advisors if they could.**

This means 1 in 4 current PhD students, looking back, chose the wrong advisor. This is not a fringe problem.

### On the role of the advisor:
> "Success in academe highly depends on lab/supervisor. A good supervisor can either make you happy during your Ph.D. journey or destroy your life/career."

> "The academic system doesn't always help the position of the Ph.D. student...good relationship with your supervisor is a necessity."

## Bullying and Harassment

- **21%** reported being bullied in their PhD programs
- Of bullied students: **48% identified their supervisor as the perpetrator**
- **Over 50%** of bullied students felt unable to speak out
- **21%** reported experiencing harassment or discrimination
- Gender discrimination most prevalent among women reporting sexual harassment

**Key implication**: Bullying by supervisors is not rare — it affects roughly 1 in 10 PhD students (21% who were bullied, ~half attributed to supervisor). Programs with no formal advisor accountability mechanisms create structural vulnerability.

## Career Uncertainty Data

- **79%** ranked job/career uncertainty in their top five concerns
- **78%** worried about work-life balance
- **74%** concerned about finishing on time
- **70%** worried about faculty research job availability

### Career aspirations vs. reality gap:
- **Over 50%** planned academic careers at time of survey
- Only **6%** expected to become an assistant professor immediately after PhD
- **46%** anticipated a postdoc position
- **46%** imagined a permanent position within 1-2 years after PhD

**Reality check**: Less than half who expect a permanent position within 2 years will achieve it (see `nsf_phd_career_outcomes_2023.txt` for the actual 20-year trend of academia shrinking from 54% to 34% of placements).

---

## Career Preparation Gaps

- **53%** cited "learning career possibilities" as the most difficult aspect of PhD planning
- **29%** received useful non-academic career advice from their program
- **25%** felt well-prepared for non-science, non-research careers
- **60%** based career plans on personal research rather than institutional guidance

---

## Work Hours and Financial Strain

- **27%** spent 41-50 hours per week on PhD work
- **25%** spent 51-60 hours per week
- **~50%** reported a "long-hours culture" in their programs
- **37%** reported good work-life balance
- **19%** held outside jobs
- **53%** of those working outside cited financial necessity as the primary reason

---

## Overall Satisfaction (The Nuanced Finding)

Despite all the above:
- **75%** were satisfied with their decision to pursue a PhD
- **71%** were satisfied with their time in program
- **8%** said they would not pursue a PhD if given a do-over

**How to read this**: High surface satisfaction coexists with serious structural problems. PhD students are socialized to find meaning in their work and are committed to their decisions — high satisfaction rates do not mean the structural problems (advisor power, mental health, career uncertainty) are solved.

---

## Implications for Grad School Decision-Making

1. **25% advisor regret rate** → Selecting an advisor is the highest-stakes decision of the PhD. "The program is good" is not sufficient due diligence.
2. **Mental health is a systemic feature, not a personal weakness** → 39% depression rate is structural, not individual. Ask about mental health resources and advisor policies before enrolling.
3. **79% career uncertainty** → Career support must be explicitly evaluated. Ask: "What percentage of graduates get faculty positions? What percentage go to industry? Who are recent graduates working for?"
4. **50% getting less than 1 hour/week with advisor** → Ask target advisor: "How often do you meet one-on-one with PhD students? What does weekly mentoring look like?"
