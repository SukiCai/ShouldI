## Overview

The NSF Survey of Earned Doctorates (SED) is an annual census of all research doctorate recipients from US universities. The 2023 survey covered 57,862 individuals at 459 institutions. 98.6% earned a PhD.

---

## Postgraduation Employment: 2023 Data

Of 41,273 responding Science & Engineering doctorate recipients:
- **74%** had definite commitments for employment or postdoctoral positions
- **26%** had no definite postgraduation plans

Non-S&E fields: comparable rate at **73%**.

---

## Employment by Sector (2023, US positions only)

Of 22,462 S&E doctorate recipients with **non-postdoc** employment commitments:

| Sector | Count | Percentage |
|--------|-------|------------|
| Industry / Business | 10,495 | **47%** |
| Academia | 7,672 | **34%** |
| Government | 1,535 | **7%** |
| Nonprofit | 1,462 | **7%** |
| Other / unreported | 1,298 | **6%** |

**Key finding**: In 2023, industry (47%) surpassed academia (34%) as the primary destination for new PhD graduates. This is now the norm, not an exception.

---

## Postdoc Rates by Field (2023)

12,818 doctorate recipients reported US postdoc positions. Postdoc rates exceeded 50% in:

| Field | Postdoc rate |
|-------|-------------|
| Geosciences, atmospheric, ocean sciences | 64.2% |
| Biological / biomedical sciences | 57.8% |
| Physical sciences | 46.5% |

**Implication**: In biology and physical sciences, the postdoc is not optional — it is a mandatory intermediate step before any permanent position, adding 2-5 years to time-to-career-stability.

---

## Definite Commitment Rates by Field

**Highest** (indicating strong immediate job markets):
- Business: **85.9%**
- Psychology: **80.0%**

**Lowest** (indicating more uncertainty at graduation):
- Humanities / arts: **65.8%** — more than 1 in 3 had no definite plans
- Biological / biomedical sciences: **68.2%**

---

## 20-Year Trend: The Dramatic Decline of Academic Placements (2003–2023)

This is the most important longitudinal finding for anyone planning an academic career:

| Field | Academic placement 2003 | Academic placement 2023 | Change |
|-------|------------------------|------------------------|--------|
| **All S&E** | **54%** | **34%** | **-20 percentage points** |
| Mathematics / statistics | 70% | 27% | -43 pp |
| Computer science | ~45% | ~20% | estimated -25 pp |
| Physical sciences | ~55% | ~35% | -20 pp |

Industry/business employment nearly doubled across most fields over the same period.

**What this means**: A PhD student entering in 2025 who assumes an academic job market similar to when their advisor graduated is working from a 20-year-old mental model. The market has fundamentally restructured.

## Field-Specific Career Realities

### Biological / Biomedical Sciences
- 57.8% go to postdocs (mandatory step)
- 68.2% have definite plans at graduation (lowest among major fields)
- Industry biotech, pharma, and research are the growing destination
- Academic tenure-track positions are highly competitive; most labs produce 10+ PhDs per tenure-track slot

### Physical Sciences (Physics, Chemistry, etc.)
- 46.5% go to postdocs
- Physics: academic placements have declined significantly; national labs and industry now absorb more graduates
- Chemistry: industry (pharma, materials science, chemicals) has grown substantially

### Computer Science / Engineering
- Lower postdoc rates than life sciences (CS → industry without postdoc is common)
- Industry destinations: software, data science, AI/ML, product roles
- Academic positions competitive but more available than life sciences per PhD produced

### Humanities and Arts
- Only 65.8% have definite plans at graduation — worst of all fields
- Long postdoc / visiting lecturer pipeline before any permanent position
- Tenure-track placements for humanities PhDs: approximately 25-30% of graduates (field varies widely)
- Many spend 3-10 years as adjuncts or visiting faculty before a tenure-track role

### Psychology
- 80.0% have definite plans at graduation — best of all fields
- Clinical psychology PhDs: licensed practice is a strong career path
- Research psychology: academic competition is intense; many move to applied industry roles

## Implications for Grad School Selection

1. **"I want a professor job"**: In most fields, this is now a minority outcome for PhD graduates. Ask your target program: "What percentage of your PhD graduates obtained tenure-track faculty positions in the last 5 years?" If the answer is above 30%, the program has above-average outcomes.

2. **Postdoc as invisible cost**: Biology PhDs must factor 3-5 years of postdoc (median salary ~$52,000-$65,000/year) before being competitive for faculty roles. The "time to independent career" for a biology PhD is effectively 10-12 years from bachelor's.

3. **20-year trend is accelerating, not reversing**: The academic job market has been shrinking for two decades. Advisors who entered academia in 2005 may not have accurate mental models of current placement realities.

4. **Field selection matters for career optionality**: CS/Engineering PhD → industry is seamless. Biology PhD → industry often requires retraining or reframing. Humanities PhD → non-academic is a cultural/identity shift that programs rarely support.

5. **"Definite commitment at graduation" is the most honest metric**: Not whether graduates "get jobs" eventually, but whether they have committed plans on graduation day. Humanities/biomedical at 65-68% means a significant minority face genuine uncertainty.
