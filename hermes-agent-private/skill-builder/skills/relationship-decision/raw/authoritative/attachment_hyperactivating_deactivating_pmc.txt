Source: PubMed Central (PMC) — "Attachment Manifestations in Daily Interpersonal Interactions" (peer-reviewed)
URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9537404/
Topic: How anxious (hyperactivating) and avoidant (deactivating) attachment strategies manifest moment-to-moment in real interactions, and why this destabilizes relationship-viability judgments
Country scope: Global
Source type: authoritative

---

## The two strategies

**Anxious attachment — hyperactivating strategies**: under interpersonal stress, anxiously attached individuals engage in "pressured attempts at approach and attachment." Notably, the research found anxious attachment states do NOT reliably predict warmer behavior even when the partner is being warm — anxious individuals sometimes respond to warmth with warmth, sometimes with withdrawal, producing an unpredictable pattern from the outside.

**Avoidant attachment — deactivating strategies**: avoidant attachment was specifically linked to "diminished expressions of warmth." When an avoidantly attached person perceives their partner as warm, they characteristically respond with LESS warmth — a protective distancing response, not a preference-based one.

## The core destabilizing finding

Attachment states "wax and wane across daily social interactions" depending on perceived partner warmth in the moment. This means an insecurely attached person's real-time assessment of "is this relationship working" fluctuates with momentary interpersonal cues, not with the actual trajectory of the relationship — someone can overestimate relationship problems during a withdrawn moment, or misread a partner's ordinary behavior through an insecurity lens, and mistake that moment-to-moment noise for a stable signal about whether to stay or leave.

## Why this matters for a relationship-decision skill

This is the direct evidence base for a claim the skill's Diagnostic Framework needs but currently lacks: that a person's felt certainty about "I should leave" or "I should stay," measured on any single day, is not a reliable data point if that person has an insecure attachment style — because the felt sense itself is generated by a moment-to-moment reactivity system, not a stable evaluation. This should push the skill toward asking about patterns over time/across multiple calm moments rather than trusting a single moment of clarity or panic.
