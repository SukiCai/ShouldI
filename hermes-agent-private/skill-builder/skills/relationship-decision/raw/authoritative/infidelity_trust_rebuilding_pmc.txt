## Core finding on the nature of trust rebuilding

Trust rebuilding after infidelity is described in the literature as slow, requiring conscious sustained effort from BOTH partners, and explicitly NOT linear — meaning setbacks during the process are expected and not evidence the process has failed.

## Concrete behavioral steps identified in the research

1. **Stopping contact with the affair partner** — the involved partner agreeing to stop seeing the affair partner and putting boundaries in place to avoid even accidental contact.
2. **Transparency in whereabouts** — concrete accountability measures (e.g., bringing children along on errands) as trust-building actions, not punitive ones.
3. **Disclosure of unexpected contact** — if accidental contact with the affair partner occurs, the involved partner discloses it proactively rather than waiting to be asked.
4. **Accountability via shared access** — during the period studied (COVID-19 lockdown), couples shifted toward monitored phone calls, texts, and video calls as a transparency mechanism.
5. **Emotionally risky behaviors as trust signals** — the involved partner showing up to scheduled difficult conversations with honesty and non-defensiveness is identified as a specific trust-building behavior, distinct from simply "not doing it again."

## What the research does NOT provide

No specific timeframe (in weeks/months) for when trust is "rebuilt" — the literature explicitly avoids a fixed timeline, treating it as a function of consistent behavior over time rather than calendar time.

## Why this matters for a relationship-decision skill

This gives concrete, checkable diagnostic questions distinct from "do you feel ready to trust again" (which is unfalsifiable in the moment): has the involved partner actually stopped contact, is there voluntary transparency happening, and has proactive disclosure of any contact occurred — these are observable behaviors a person can point to as evidence, rather than a feeling they're being asked to will into existence.
