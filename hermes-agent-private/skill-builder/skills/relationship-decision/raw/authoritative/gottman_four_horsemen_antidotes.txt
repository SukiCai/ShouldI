## The core research finding

Gottman's longitudinal research on couples could predict divorce with over 90% accuracy across studies, using: the ratio of positive to negative interactions, presence of the "Four Horsemen" communication patterns, and physiological measures during conflict. Critically: statistically, a marriage CAN survive the Four Horsemen — but only if partners learn to repair effectively. The consistent FAILURE of repair attempts (not the presence of conflict itself) is what predicts an unhappy future.

## The Four Horsemen and their antidotes

1. **Criticism** → Antidote: Gentle Start-Up. Address specific behaviors, not character. "I" statements over "you" statements. Example: not "You always talk about yourself, why are you so selfish?" but "I'm feeling left out and need to vent — can we talk about my day?"

2. **Contempt** → Antidote: Build a Culture of Appreciation. Regular expressions of gratitude/affection ("Small Things Often"), maintaining the "5:1 magic ratio" of positive to negative interactions. Gottman's research finding: contempt is "the greatest predictor of divorce" — more than any other single factor.

3. **Defensiveness** → Antidote: Take Responsibility. Accept partial accountability rather than positioning as victim or counter-blaming. Example: acknowledging "you're right" about a valid point while still expressing one's own perspective.

4. **Stonewalling** → Antidote: Physiological Self-Soothing. A timeout of at least 20 minutes when flooded, avoiding "righteous indignation" rumination during the break. Research finding: couples who took ~30-minute discussion breaks showed significantly lower heart rates and more positive subsequent interactions.

## Why this matters for a relationship-decision skill

This gives a concrete, evidence-anchored diagnostic: it is not "is there conflict" (all relationships have conflict) but "is there contempt specifically, and are repair attempts succeeding or consistently failing." A skill should be able to ask "when you try to repair after a fight, does it usually work, or does it usually fail the same way?" as a sharper diagnostic than a generic "are you happy" question.
