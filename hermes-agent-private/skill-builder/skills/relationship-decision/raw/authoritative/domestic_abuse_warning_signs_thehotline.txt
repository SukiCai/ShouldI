## Enumerated warning signs (this is the canonical list this skill should recognize, not paraphrase loosely)

1. Criticism — "telling you that you never do anything right"
2. Extreme jealousy about friendships or personal time
3. Isolation — discouraging or preventing contact with friends, family, peers
4. Emotional abuse — insults, demeaning language, public shaming
5. Decision control — preventing autonomous choices about work or education
6. Financial control — managing household finances without consultation, or withholding necessary funds
7. Sexual coercion — pressuring involvement in unwanted sexual acts
8. Substance pressure — pushing drug or alcohol use
9. Intimidation — threatening looks or actions to instill fear
10. Threats toward children or pets
11. Weapon intimidation
12. Property destruction — damaging belongings or the home

**Explicit threshold stated by the source**: "even one or two of these behaviors in a relationship is a red flag that abuse may be present" — i.e., this is not a checklist requiring multiple hits before it counts.

## Referral resources (the actual action, not a diagnosis this skill should attempt itself)

- Call 1-800-799-SAFE (7233)
- Text "START" to 88788
- Live chat with an advocate at thehotline.org
- "Plan for Safety" tools on the same site

## Why this matters for a relationship-decision skill — this is a hard boundary, not a data point

If a user's answers surface even one or two of the items above, this is NOT a "repairability" conversation to run the standard diagnostic framework on. The skill should recognize this pattern and redirect to the hotline/professional resource rather than continuing a decision-framework interview — attempting to weigh "repairable vs. not" via the standard heuristics when active abuse is present risks normalizing it as a garden-variety relationship problem.
