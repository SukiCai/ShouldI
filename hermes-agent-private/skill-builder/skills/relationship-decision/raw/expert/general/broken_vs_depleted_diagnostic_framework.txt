## Step 1: Distinguish Depleted vs. Broken

**Depleted relationships**: lack fuel but retain love. Partners feel exhausted from external life stressors, not from structural damage to the relationship itself.

**Broken relationships**: have fractured foundations — betrayal, abuse, contempt, or fundamental incompatibility at the core.

O'Sullivan's diagnostic questions to distinguish the two:
- "Do I still feel safe with this person, even when I'm angry?"
- "When I imagine my partner at their best, do I still want that person?"
- "Is the pain from what's missing (depletion) or from what's been done (breakage)?"

## Step 2: The Connection First Protocol (repair sequence — order matters)

1. Safety (emotional and physical) — must be established first
2. Connection — reconnect emotionally before attempting to resolve the substantive issue
3. Cognitive access — only once safety and connection are present does the "thinking brain" become available
4. Problem solving — the substantive conversation happens last, not first

The clinical claim here is that couples who try to jump straight to "problem solving" while emotional safety is absent will fail, regardless of how good their proposed solution is.

## When to let go (explicit criteria, not vague)

- One or both partners lack willingness to try
- Active abuse exists (physical, emotional, financial, or sexual)
- "Contempt has replaced conflict" — echoes Gottman's finding that contempt specifically predicts failure
- Core values are fundamentally incompatible
- Only one partner is doing the repair work

## When it's worth fighting for

Both partners remain willing, the pain stems from depletion (not breakage), the good years outweigh the bad, and both partners are actively pursuing help (not just one).

## Why this matters for a relationship-decision skill

This is the closest thing in the source material to a ready-made decision tree: "depleted vs. broken" as the first fork, then the explicit "when to let go" list as a checkable set of conditions rather than a feeling. It should anchor the skill's core diagnostic step, with Gottman's Four Horsemen as the conflict-pattern-level detail underneath it and the thehotline.org warning-signs list as the hard override that pre-empts this framework entirely when triggered.
