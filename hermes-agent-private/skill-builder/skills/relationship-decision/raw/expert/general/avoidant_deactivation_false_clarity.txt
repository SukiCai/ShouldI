## The core mechanism: manufactured reasons

When emotional intimacy intensifies, avoidantly attached individuals may "look for petty reasons to end a relationship — such as a partner's inconsequential actions, appearance, or slightly annoying habits." This is a deactivating strategy: the attachment system responds to growing closeness by triggering withdrawal, and the person experiencing it interprets the withdrawal as evidence of genuine incompatibility rather than recognizing it as an anxiety response to intimacy itself.

## Specific triggers that get misread as "this isn't working"

- A partner pushing for closeness or intimacy
- Feeling required to be dependent on someone
- The relationship "taking up too much time"

Each of these produces pulling-away behavior that "feels authentic to the person experiencing it, even though it's a defensive response rather than true preference."

## The anxious-avoidant reinforcement loop

An anxiously attached partner "may struggle to understand an avoidant partner's actions and push for closeness" — which is precisely the trigger that intensifies avoidant withdrawal, which then confirms the anxious partner's fear of abandonment, producing an escalating push-pull cycle where neither partner's read on "is this relationship working" reflects the relationship's actual state.

## Why this matters for a relationship-decision skill — the exact gap this fills

This is the missing piece for a claim the skill needs to make explicitly: **feeling certain you want to leave is not the same as the relationship actually being unrepairable, if that certainty arrived right after a spike in closeness.** A diagnostic question this source supports: "did the urge to end things follow a moment of increased closeness or vulnerability, or did it build gradually and independently of that?" — the former pattern is consistent with deactivation-as-false-clarity; the latter is not. This should sit alongside (not replace) the existing Broken-vs-Depleted framework as a check specifically for avoidant-pattern users, the same way the PMC hyperactivating/deactivating file supports a parallel check for anxious-pattern users.
