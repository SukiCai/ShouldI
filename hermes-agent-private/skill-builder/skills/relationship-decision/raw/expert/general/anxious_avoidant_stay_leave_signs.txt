## Signs pointing toward leaving

- Ongoing anxiety or emotional triggers that persist rather than resolve
- One partner unwilling to work on the recurring pattern
- Repeated conflict without progress over time
- Emotional withdrawal that never gets resolved

## The reframe on leaving

"Leaving doesn't mean the relationship didn't matter; it may mean it's not meeting your needs" — offered explicitly as a counter to the common distortion that ending an anxious-avoidant relationship is a verdict on its past validity, which is itself a piece of framing worth using directly with a user stuck on "but we had good years."

## What this source does NOT provide (be explicit about the gap)

This source gives outcome-level signs (ongoing anxiety, no progress, no resolution) but does not decompose *why* those signs might be attachment-driven rather than relationship-driven — it treats "mutual willingness to change and consistency over time" as the load-bearing test, without separately asking whether one partner's apparent unwillingness is actually deactivation (see the companion attachmentproject.com file) or whether the "ongoing anxiety" is hyperactivation reading ordinary distance as threat (see the companion PMC file).

## Why this matters for a relationship-decision skill

Use this as the outcome-level check (has enough time passed with visible non-improvement) layered on top of, not instead of, the attachment-mechanism checks from the other two attachment sources — a skill that only asks "has this been going on a while with no progress" will still misfire on a couple where the "no progress" is actually one partner's deactivation cycle resetting every time things get close.
