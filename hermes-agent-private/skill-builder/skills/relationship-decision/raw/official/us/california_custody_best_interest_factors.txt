## Enumerated factors

When determining custody, California judges consider:

1. The age and health of the child
2. The emotional ties between the parents and the child
3. The child's ties to their school, home, and community
4. The ability of each parent to care for the child
5. Any history of family violence
6. Any regular and ongoing substance abuse by either parent

This is the state-law implementation (California Family Code §3011) of the "best interests of the child" standard that all US states use in some form, though the specific enumerated factors vary by state.

## Why this matters for a relationship-decision skill

When a user with children is weighing whether to leave, "best interest of the child" is not an abstract phrase — it is the literal legal standard a court applies, built from concrete, checkable factors (school/community ties, existing emotional bonds, any documented violence or substance abuse). A skill discussing "how leaving affects the kids" should be able to connect the emotional conversation to what a court would actually weigh, rather than treating "the children" as an undifferentiated variable. Family violence and substance abuse are explicit aggravating factors under the same standard — this is also where this section connects back to the abuse-screening override established elsewhere in this skill: documented family violence doesn't just change the relationship calculus, it changes the custody calculus too.
