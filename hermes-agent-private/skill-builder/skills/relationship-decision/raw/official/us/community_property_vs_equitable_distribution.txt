## The two regimes

**Community property**: "Assets acquired during a marriage by either spouse" — property, income, and even debt. In a community property state, both spouses own everything acquired during the marriage equally (50/50), regardless of whose name is on it or who earned the income. Property owned before the marriage, or received individually as a gift/inheritance, is generally separate property and excluded.

**Community property states**: Arizona, California, Idaho, Louisiana, Nevada, New Mexico, Texas, Washington, Wisconsin. Alaska is opt-in (couples can elect community property treatment by agreement).

**Equitable distribution** (the majority-state model, used everywhere else in the US): courts divide marital property based on a list of fairness factors set by state law, not a mechanical 50/50 split. "Equitable" is explicitly distinct from "equal" — a longer marriage, income disparity, or non-financial contributions (e.g., homemaking, supporting a spouse's career) can shift the split away from 50/50.

## Why this matters for a relationship-decision skill

Which regime applies is a structural fact that changes the stakes of "how" someone leaves, independent of the emotional merits of leaving. A person in a community property state has a mechanically predictable outcome (50/50 of marital assets); a person in an equitable-distribution state is entering a more negotiated, fact-specific process. This is a checkable fact (which state) that should be surfaced once "leaving" becomes the live path — the skill should be able to name which regime applies rather than giving regime-agnostic property advice.
