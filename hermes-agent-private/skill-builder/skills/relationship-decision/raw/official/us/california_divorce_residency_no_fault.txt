## Residency requirement

To file for divorce in California, one spouse must have:
- Lived in California for the last 6 months, AND
- Resided in their current California county for the last 3 months

If neither spouse meets this, they generally must wait until the requirement is met before filing in California.

## No-fault filing

California does not require the filing spouse to prove wrongdoing. Per the official guidance: "You don't need your spouse's agreement to get a divorce. You can get divorced for any reason." This is the no-fault model most US states have converged on, though the specific residency window (commonly 3-12 months depending on state) and terminology ("dissolution of marriage" vs. "divorce") vary by state.

## Practical implication for decision framing

Because residency requirements exist, someone recently relocated to a new state (e.g., after a job move, or an international student/professional on a US visa who changed states) may not be immediately eligible to file locally — this is a concrete, checkable fact that should be surfaced before any conversation about "how" to leave, not just "whether" to leave.

## Why this matters for a relationship-decision skill

Legal mechanics (residency windows, no-fault vs. fault framing) are a separate axis from the emotional/repairability diagnosis. A user asking "should I leave" and a user asking "how do I actually leave, given I just moved states" are asking different questions that require different information — this skill should not conflate them.
