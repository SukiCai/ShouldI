## The 2-year threshold

BC's Family Law Act extends the same property-division rights married couples have to unmarried couples once they have "lived together in a marriage-like relationship for at least two years." Below two years, no statutory property-division right exists by default.

## What counts as family property

Once the threshold is met, common-law partners "will generally share any property they acquire during the course of their relationship — but not property brought into the relationship." Pre-relationship assets stay separate; assets accumulated together during the relationship are divided.

## Opt-out

Couples can contract out of these default rules entirely via a written agreement if they don't want the statutory property-division rules to apply.

## Historical context (why this exists)

Before 2013, unmarried couples in BC had no statutory right to court-ordered property division at all, which produced unpredictable, expensive litigation. The reform reflected the reality that common-law families were becoming common and often functioned identically to married families.

## Why this matters for a relationship-decision skill — province variance is the key fact

This is dramatically different from Ontario (see companion file): in BC, an unmarried couple past the 2-year mark has essentially the same property rights as a married couple. In Ontario, common-law couples never get automatic property division regardless of duration. A user asking "if I leave, what happens to our stuff" cannot be answered generically for "Canada" — province is a required diagnostic fact once someone is unmarried and considering leaving, in the same way US state is required for community-property-vs-equitable-distribution.
