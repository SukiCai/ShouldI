## Married couples: equalization, not a physical 50/50 split

"In Ontario, property acquired during a marriage must be split equally when a marriage ends for any reason." This is implemented as an "equalization payment" (equalization of net family property), calculated as:

1. Each spouse's property value at separation minus debts and excluded property
2. Minus each spouse's pre-marriage property value (minus pre-marriage debts)
3. The difference between the two spouses' resulting net family property figures
4. The spouse with the larger net family property pays half the difference to the other

Critically, the family home is treated specially: "You must share the full value of the family home, even if" one spouse owned it before the marriage, or received it as a gift or inheritance — the usual pre-marriage/gift exclusions do not apply to the matrimonial home.

**Time limit**: 6 years from separation, or 2 years from the finalized divorce (whichever comes first), to bring a court claim for the equalization amount.

## Common-law couples: no automatic division

"Common law couples are not legally required to split property acquired when they lived together." Each partner keeps what they individually purchased, full stop — unlike BC, there is no duration threshold (2 years or otherwise) that converts a common-law couple into one with automatic property-division rights in Ontario. Voluntary cohabitation agreements are the only mechanism for common-law partners to establish property terms.

## Why this matters for a relationship-decision skill

This is the sharpest contrast case for "province matters" (paired with the BC file): the same fact pattern — unmarried couple, together 5+ years, separating — produces automatic 50/50-equivalent division in BC and *zero* automatic division in Ontario. A skill giving property guidance without first establishing province (for Canada) or state (for the US) is not just imprecise, it can be actively wrong in the opposite direction depending on which jurisdiction the user is actually in.
