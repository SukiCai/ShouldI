OCCUPATIONAL CLASSIFICATION FOR PRODUCT MANAGERS

The Bureau of Labor Statistics does not have a standalone "Product Manager" occupational code. PM roles are captured primarily under:

SOC 11-3021 Computer and Information Systems Managers
- Median annual wage (May 2023): $169,510
- Employment (May 2023): 530,000 workers
- Projected growth 2022–2032: +15% (much faster than average)
- Annual job openings projected: 46,800 (includes replacement)

SOC 11-1021 General and Operations Managers
- Median annual wage: $123,370
- Broader classification; some PM roles at non-tech companies file here

SOC 11-2021 Marketing Managers
- Used for some growth PM / product marketing manager roles
- Median annual wage: $140,040

For H-1B filing purposes, most tech PMs file under SOC 11-3021 (Computer and Information Systems Managers).

EMPLOYMENT PROJECTIONS 2022–2032

SOC 11-3021 Computer and Information Systems Managers:
- Projected employment 2032: 609,500
- Change 2022–2032: +79,200 new jobs (+15%)
- Annual openings (new + replacement): 46,800/year

Driving factors per BLS:
1. Continued expansion of cloud computing and data analytics
2. Increasing cybersecurity needs creating management demand
3. Growth in AI/ML product development requiring technical managers
4. Remote work normalization expanding geographic hiring pools

Factors creating uncertainty:
- Automation of some project management functions via AI tools
- Macroeconomic cycles affecting tech hiring (2022–2023 layoffs reduced employment temporarily)
- Offshoring of some product roles to lower-cost markets

Prevailing wage levels are set by the Department of Labor's Foreign Labor Certification (FLC) Data Center for each occupation × location combination. The four wage levels for Computer and Information Systems Managers in major tech markets (approximate 2024–2025):

New York City:
- Level I: ~$159,000

Austin, TX:
- Level I: ~$130,000

These thresholds determine lottery entries under the wage-weighted H-1B system (effective February 2026): a PM at Level IV receives 4 lottery entries vs. 1 for Level I.

CAREER PATHWAY CONTEXT (FROM BLS OOH)

Typical entry paths per BLS:
- Most computer and information systems managers have a bachelor's degree in CS, IT, or related field
- Work experience: 3–5 years required (in subordinate roles) before typical management appointment
- MBA increasingly common for senior PM leadership roles

Skills emphasized by BLS as job requirements:
- Communication (cross-functional coordination)
- Analytical skills (interpreting technical performance data)
- Decision-making (prioritization under resource constraints)
- Leadership and organizational skills

The BLS OOH notes that employers increasingly prefer candidates who combine technical background with business training — consistent with the "business-technical bridge" framing of PM roles.

INDUSTRY DISTRIBUTION (SOC 11-3021)

Top industries employing Computer and Information Systems Managers:
1. Professional, scientific, and technical services: 24% of employment
2. Finance and insurance: 17%
3. Manufacturing: 10%
4. Information: 9% (tech companies proper)
5. Government: 8%

Note: The "Information" industry sector (pure tech companies) has a smaller employment share than perception suggests because many PM and engineering management roles at tech companies are coded across multiple industry sectors.

===

DATA FRESHNESS NOTE

BLS OEWS data is collected in May each year and published approximately 12–14 months later. May 2023 data (published March 2024) is the most recent publicly available as of early 2026. For real-time compensation benchmarking, supplement with levels.fyi and Glassdoor (see authoritative files). BLS data is best used for trend direction, geographic differentials, and occupational projections — not for offer benchmarking.
