THE CORE PROBLEM

"Senior PM" at a mid-tier startup and "Senior PM" at Google are not equivalent roles. Titles are internally defined and not portable across company boundaries. When switching companies, accepting the same title may be a downlevel, while accepting a lower title may be a promotion in disguise.

The skill is reading what the level actually means in terms of scope, autonomy, and compensation — not what it says on the offer letter.

===

HOW MAJOR COMPANIES STRUCTURE PM LEVELS

GOOGLE (L-codes)
- L3: Associate PM / APM (fresh grad, rotational program)
- L4: Product Manager (early career, executes on defined scope)
- L5: Senior Product Manager (owns a product area, drives strategy)
- L6: Staff Product Manager / Group PM (leads multiple PMs or complex cross-team area)
- L7: Principal Product Manager (senior IC) or Director (management track fork)
- L8+: Senior Director / VP

"Senior PM" at Google = L5. Google inflates the "Senior" label — Google L5 is roughly equivalent to what other companies call "mid-level" in scope.

META (E-codes / IC-codes)
- IC3: APM / PM (entry-level)
- IC4: PM (early career)
- IC5: Senior PM (their mid-level; wide scope but not the most senior individual contributor)
- IC6: Staff PM (high-scope IC; approximately equivalent to Google L6)
- IC7: Principal PM (very senior IC, rare)
- Management fork: IC5/IC6 can move to M1 (Manager), M2 (Senior Manager), M3 (Director)

META E5 = IC5 = Senior PM at Meta. Comparable in scope and comp to Google L5.

AMAZON (L-codes — inflated scale)
- L5: Product Manager (Amazon's baseline; roughly mid-level at other companies)
- L6: Senior Product Manager (Amazon L6 is considered equivalent to Senior PM at Google/Meta in scope)
- L7: Senior Product Manager Technical (SPMT) or Senior PM II (high scope)
- L8: Principal PM / Director
- L10+: VP

Amazon L6 ≈ Google L5 ≈ Meta IC5 in scope (Amazon's L-numbers run inflated relative to FAANG peers). A Senior PM at Amazon is L6, not L5.

Key cross-company mapping:
- Amazon L5 ≈ Google L4 / Meta IC4 (mid-career PM)
- Amazon L6 ≈ Google L5 / Meta IC5 (senior PM)
- Amazon L7 ≈ Google L6 / Meta IC6 (staff/principal)

MICROSOFT (L-codes, 60–80 scale)
- L59–L62: PM / PM2 (early career)
- L63–L64: Senior PM (mid to senior)
- L65–L67: Principal PM (senior IC track)
- L67+: Partner / Partner PM (high-scope IC equivalent to GPM at other companies)
- L68+: Director / General Manager (management track)

Microsoft L63 ≈ Amazon L6 ≈ Google L5 in scope terms.

APPLE (ICT-codes)
- ICT2–ICT3: APM / PM (entry to mid)
- ICT4: Senior PM (Apple's "Senior"; comparable to Google L5)
- ICT5: Staff/Principal PM equivalent
- ICT6+: Director level

Apple ICT4 ≈ Google L5 ≈ Meta IC5 ≈ Amazon L6 in senior PM scope.

===

LEVELING AT STARTUPS (THE TITLE INFLATION PROBLEM)

Startups have extreme title inflation because they can't compete on cash comp:
- A "Director of Product" at a Series A startup may own less scope than a Senior PM at Google
- A "VP of Product" at a 30-person startup may have less organizational reach than a L6 Staff PM at Meta
- The equity upside is the intended compensation, but equity value is highly uncertain

When evaluating a startup offer:
- Ignore the title entirely
- Ask: "What will I own? What decisions can I make unilaterally? Who has override authority?"
- If the answer is "you own the full product roadmap with no PM above you," that's high scope — treat as equivalent to a principal/staff IC role
- If the answer is "you'll work under the CEO's product direction," that's lower scope than the title suggests

===

ACCEPTING A DOWNLEVEL: WHEN IT MAKES SENSE

Legitimate reasons to accept a downlevel (lower title) when switching companies:

1. Tier jump: Moving from a mid-tier company to a top-tier FAANG as a GPM but accepting Senior PM offers the brand, network, and compensation upside that more than compensates
   - Example: Accepting L5 (Senior PM) at Google from a GPM role at a mid-market B2B company — total comp increase from ~$200K to ~$400K+ justifies the title step-down

2. Scope jump: A higher level at your current company means less than a lower level at the target company
   - A Principal PM at a 50-person startup may have less cross-company influence than an L5 at Google

3. Track change: Moving from management back to IC, which requires accepting a senior IC level while rebuilding product work track record

Reasons NOT to accept a downlevel:
- When the comp at the lower level is less than what you'd earn on your current trajectory within 12 months
- When the "scope" at the new level is genuinely smaller despite the brand upgrade
- When the downlevel isn't tied to a defined upgrade path with stated timeline

===

HOW TO READ LEVELING SIGNALS IN AN OFFER

Questions to ask before accepting:
1. "What level is this role, and what's the level code?" (Don't accept titles alone — get the code)
2. "What is the promotion criterion from this level to the next, and what's the typical time-to-promo?"
3. "What level are the PMs I'd work with / manage?"
4. "What level did the last person in this role hold?"

Compensation tells you the real level:
- Use levels.fyi to verify the compensation is consistent with the stated level
- If a company says "Senior PM" but the total comp offer is $180K, that's below the median for Senior PM at that company — flag it
- Levels.fyi median total comp benchmarks (2024–2025):
  - Google Senior PM (L5): ~$528K median total comp
  - Meta Senior PM (IC5): ~$400–600K range
  - Amazon Senior PM (L6): ~$300–400K range
  - Microsoft Senior PM (L63): ~$250–350K range
  - General PM market median: ~$229K (includes all company tiers)

===

THE LATERAL THAT'S ACTUALLY A PROMOTION (AND VICE VERSA)

Lateral in title = promotion if:
- Compensation increases significantly (>20% total comp)
- Scope is materially larger (bigger team, more complex product, more autonomy)
- Brand of company opens more future doors

Promotion in title = lateral or even downgrade if:
- You're being leveled up at a company where the next level has the same scope as your current role (title bump without scope increase)
- The promotion comes without comp increase ("we value you, but budget is tight")
- The elevated title makes you look "overleveled" for the roles you'd want to take next

The diagnostic: Is this move a stepping stone or a plateau? If a hiring manager at your next target company would look at your new title and comp and say "this is a more qualified candidate," it's a real step up. If they'd say "interesting, but what was the actual scope?", the title hasn't done work.
