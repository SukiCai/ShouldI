THE CANADIAN CONTEXT IS FUNDAMENTALLY DIFFERENT FROM THE US CONTEXT

Most stay-or-return frameworks are written for US H-1B holders. The Canadian immigrant experience has structural differences that change almost every part of the framework:

1. IMMIGRATION SECURITY: Canadian PR is achievable in 1–2 years via Express Entry vs. 134-year US EB-2 backlog for Indian nationals. The "visa precarity" driver that pushes many US H-1B holders toward returning early is substantially reduced or absent for Canadian PR holders.

2. HEALTHCARE: Canada's universal healthcare system removes a major financial calculation from the return decision. US H-1B holders often stay partially because employer-sponsored health insurance is difficult to replicate after returning home. Canadian PR holders don't have this anchor.

3. SALARY DIFFERENTIAL: Canadian tech salaries are lower than US tech salaries in USD terms — typically 25–40% lower for comparable roles. The financial case for staying in Canada is weaker than for staying in the US. This is partly offset by lower healthcare costs and more predictable immigration path.

4. GEOGRAPHIC FLEXIBILITY: Canadian PR holders can move between provinces without immigration implications. This is not the same as the US H-1B, which is employer-tied and location-tied.

===

WHO IS ASKING THE STAY-OR-RETURN QUESTION IN CANADA

Three main profiles:

Profile A: International student who completed Canadian degree + PGWP
- Typical path: 4-year undergraduate in Canada + PGWP (up to 3 years) → CEC Express Entry PR
- By the time they're asking the stay-or-return question, they're typically 26–30 years old, early in career, and may have Canadian PR or PR application in process
- The question is often: "I have Canadian PR — do I stay in Canada long-term, return home, or transition to the US?"

Profile B: Experienced worker who moved from India/China to Canada with tech job (Express Entry)
- Typically 30–40 years old, mid-career, moved directly with family
- Asking: "We're established here, but [aging parents / home country opportunity / income differential with US] is making us reconsider"

Profile C: US worker considering Canada as an intermediate step
- Was on US H-1B, considering Canada as a way to get immigration security before returning to home country or staying in North America
- Canada PR as a hedge: "Get Canadian PR → return to India/China if home country pans out → re-enter Canada or use as global mobility base if not"

===

CANADIAN PR VS. US GREEN CARD: KEY DIFFERENCES FOR STAY-OR-RETURN

Residency obligation:
- Canada PR: Must be physically in Canada for 730 days out of every 5 years (the "2-in-5" rule)
- US Green Card: Must maintain US domicile; extended absences (6+ months) can trigger questioning at reentry; 1+ year without re-entry permit is considered abandonment

Flexibility for return trips:
- Canadian PR holder can live in home country for up to 3 consecutive years without losing PR — IF they return to Canada at least once during that 5-year window and meet the 730-day requirement overall
- US Green Card holder cannot realistically be absent for 3 years without significant risk of abandonment finding at border

Citizenship timeline:
- Canada: 3 years of physical presence in Canada within 5 years (730+ days)
- US: 5 continuous years of LPR status + physical presence requirements + prior to applying (for most categories)

Dual citizenship:
- Canada: YES, allows dual citizenship
- US: Technically discourages (oath language) but practically allows; IRS worldwide taxation follows the person regardless

Canadian citizenship value: Canadian passport ranks among top 5–10 globally for visa-free access (visa-free/on-arrival to 185+ countries). For Indian and Chinese nationals, this is significantly more valuable than their home country passport.

THE CANADIAN STAY-OR-RETURN FINANCIAL CALCULATION

Model: Indian tech professional in Toronto, 5 years in Canada

Typical profile:
- Software Engineer / PM, 5 years experience
- Gross income: CAD $130,000/year ($95,000 USD)
- Marital status: Married, one child
- Housing: Renting 2BR in Toronto near transit at CAD $2,800/month

Annual income breakdown:
- Gross: CAD $130,000
- Federal + Ontario tax (with standard deductions): ~$44,000
- Net take-home: ~$86,000 (CAD)
- Monthly net: ~$7,167

Monthly expenses (Toronto):
- Rent: $2,800
- Groceries: $600
- Transportation (transit + occasional car): $300
- Utilities + phone + internet: $350
- Childcare (CWELCC subsidized): $700–$1,000 (subsidy reducing from market rate of $2,000+)
- Other (clothing, dining, personal): $700
- Total: ~$5,450–$5,750

Monthly savings: $1,400–$1,700 (CAD)
Annual savings: ~$18,000–$20,000 (CAD) / ~$13,000–$15,000 (USD)

Comparison to equivalent US position:
- Comparable Senior SWE/PM in Seattle: $200,000+ total comp
- After Washington State taxes (no income tax) and higher healthcare/living costs: net savings often $50,000–$80,000/year
- The Canada-US savings differential for comparable role is approximately 3–5× in favor of staying in the US

WHEN CANADA MAKES SENSE AS THE RIGHT HOME

Canada is the better choice (over both US and returning home) when:

1. You have Canadian PR and immigration security matters more than maximizing income
2. You have family already settled in Canada (partner has established career; children in school)
3. You're from a field where Canada has strong demand and compensation is less dramatically different (some healthcare, finance, government roles)
4. Your partner cannot qualify for US visa/work authorization but can work freely in Canada
5. You value universal healthcare, lower education costs for children, and social safety net over income maximization
6. You're considering the Canada→citizenship→global optionality path (Canadian passport as mobility asset)

Return home is more compelling from Canada when:
1. Parents are aging and care needs are real (proximity matters; Canada doesn't solve the distance)
2. Your savings rate in Canada is low enough that the home-country savings rate is comparable in PPP terms
3. You have a concrete home-country opportunity (not just the idea of one)
4. You're on PGWP without yet securing PR (immigration uncertainty remains)
5. You haven't built strong local network in Canada yet

===

THE CANADA → HOME COUNTRY → BACK TO CANADA PATH

This is a realistic circular path that Canadian immigration policy allows:

Step 1: Secure Canadian PR (Express Entry, typically 1–2 years from initial application)
Step 2: Return to India/China for 2–5 years (work, eldercare, entrepreneurship, etc.)
Step 3: Maintain 730-day requirement by returning to Canada periodically (or retain exemptions for PR abroad with Canadian citizen spouse)
Step 4: Apply for Canadian citizenship after meeting physical presence requirement, OR maintain PR and eventually return to Canada

This circular path is not available from the US: US Green Card abandonment with return would require reapplying from scratch. Canadian PR is structurally more flexible.

Practical note: To actually execute this, the PR holder needs to:
- Track days of physical presence carefully (Service Canada has a travel history tool)
- Be aware that extended absences may create questions at border on return
- Not start a job outside Canada for the full 5-year period without ensuring return to meet 730 days

===

DIAGNOSTIC QUESTIONS FOR CANADIAN-BASED USERS

1. "What is your current immigration status in Canada, and what is the status of your PR application?"
2. "If you have PR, have you done the 730-day calculation for the current 5-year window?"
3. "What is your current savings rate in CAD, and what would it be if you returned home?"
4. "Is your partner able to work in Canada, and what is their visa status?"
5. "Have you seriously explored the BC PNP Tech or Ontario HCP stream if you don't yet have PR?"
6. "Are you comparing Canada to the US, or Canada to returning home — these are different decisions"
7. "What does Canadian citizenship give you that returning now doesn't?"
