WHY A CANADA-SPECIFIC MODEL IS NECESSARY

The stay-or-return savings model for US-based users assumes high US tech compensation, high but manageable US costs, and very high US taxes. Canadian numbers are structurally different:

- CAD salaries are 25–40% lower than USD equivalents for the same role
- Canadian federal + provincial taxes are higher than most US states
- Healthcare is universal (no health insurance premium deductions from take-home)
- Housing in Toronto/Vancouver is among the most expensive in North America
- Childcare costs are lower (CWELCC subsidized to $10/day in many provinces)

Without a Canada-specific model, an advisor applying the US model will produce wrong conclusions about whether staying in Canada beats returning home.

MODEL 1: TORONTO — SOFTWARE ENGINEER, SINGLE, 3 YEARS EXPERIENCE

Input assumptions:
- Gross income: CAD $105,000/year
- Status: Renting solo
- No children
- Province: Ontario

Step 1: Gross income
CAD $105,000

Step 2: Federal + Ontario income tax + CPP + EI
- Federal tax: ~$20,300
- Ontario tax: ~$14,900
- CPP contributions: ~$3,300
- EI premiums: ~$1,050
- Total deductions: ~$39,550
- Net take-home: ~$65,450/year = ~$5,454/month

Step 3: Monthly expenses (Toronto, solo, renting)
- Rent (1BR, mid-city): $2,200
- Groceries: $400
- Transportation (TTC monthly + occasional): $200
- Utilities + internet + phone: $250
- Dining + social: $400
- Clothing + personal: $200
- Entertainment + subscriptions: $100
- Total: $3,750

Step 4: Monthly savings
$5,454 − $3,750 = $1,704/month

Step 5: Annual savings (CAD)
$1,704 × 12 = ~$20,450/year

Step 6: USD equivalent
At CAD/USD rate of ~0.73: approximately $14,900/year USD

Step 7: PPP context (India comparison)
Equivalent purchasing power in India: $14,900 USD buys approximately ₹12.4L at market exchange rate. A mid-level software engineer in Bangalore earning ₹25L gross with ~₹18L net take-home would save ₹8L–₹12L after Bangalore expenses. In PPP-adjusted savings, Toronto and Bangalore are closer than the raw numbers suggest.

Conclusion: Single early-career professional in Toronto has moderate savings; marginally better in PPP terms than Bangalore but not dramatically so. Immigration security (PR pathway) is the primary argument for staying in Canada at this income level.

MODEL 2: TORONTO — SENIOR PM / ENGINEER, FAMILY (2 ADULTS, 1 CHILD)

Input assumptions:
- Gross household income: CAD $190,000 (one earner at $130K + partner at $60K)
- Province: Ontario
- Child, 3 years old (using CWELCC childcare subsidy at ~$10/day = ~$200/month)

Step 1: Combined gross income
CAD $190,000

Step 2: Combined taxes (approximately)
- Primary earner ($130K): Federal ~$29,000 + Ontario ~$21,000 + CPP $3,300 + EI $1,050 = ~$54,350
- Secondary earner ($60K): Federal ~$10,500 + Ontario ~$7,800 + CPP $3,300 + EI $1,050 = ~$22,650
- Total household deductions: ~$77,000
- Combined net: ~$113,000/year = ~$9,417/month

Step 3: Monthly expenses
- Rent (2BR, Toronto proper): $2,800–$3,200 (using $3,000)
- Groceries (family of 3): $700
- Transportation (TTC + 1 vehicle): $650
- Utilities + internet + phone (2): $420
- Childcare (subsidized): $200
- Children's activities + clothing: $300
- Personal + dining: $500
- Household (repairs, furnishings): $200
- Total: $5,970

Step 4: Monthly savings
$9,417 − $5,970 = $3,447/month

Step 5: Annual savings (CAD)
$3,447 × 12 = ~$41,364/year

Step 6: USD equivalent
~$30,200/year USD

Step 7: PPP context (India comparison)
A dual-income tech household in Bangalore or Hyderabad earning ₹50L–₹70L combined might save ₹15L–₹25L/year after expenses. At PPP adjustment, this is comparable to the Canadian household's savings in PPP terms.

Conclusion: Dual-income family in Toronto achieves moderate savings. The immigration security (both have Canadian PR), universal healthcare, and CWELCC childcare subsidy are significant quality-of-life benefits. But the savings rate is not dramatically superior to a strong home-country career in PPP terms. The key differentiator is: what happens to this household's trajectory over 10 years?

Input assumptions:
- Gross: CAD $155,000 (single earner)
- Province: BC
- Two children under 6; one partner not working
- Housing: Renting 3BR in Vancouver suburb ($3,200/month)

Step 1: Gross
CAD $155,000

Step 2: Federal + BC tax
- Federal: ~$39,200
- BC: ~$24,500
- CPP: ~$3,300
- EI: ~$1,050
- Total: ~$68,050
- Net: ~$86,950/year = ~$7,246/month

Step 3: Monthly expenses
- Rent (3BR, Burnaby/Surrey/Coquitlam): $3,200
- Groceries (family of 4): $900
- Transportation (1 vehicle + occasional transit): $700
- Utilities + internet + phone: $400
- Childcare (2 children subsidized): $400
- Children's activities + clothing: $500
- Personal + dining: $400
- Household: $200
- Total: $6,700

Step 4: Monthly savings
$7,246 − $6,700 = $546/month

Step 5: Annual savings (CAD)
$546 × 12 = ~$6,550/year USD equivalent: ~$4,800

CRITICAL FLAG: This single-income family in Vancouver is effectively not accumulating meaningful savings. Housing absorbs the majority of the budget. This is a strong signal that:
1. This family needs a second income to make Canada financially viable
2. Or they need to relocate to a lower-cost Canadian city (Edmonton, Calgary, Saskatoon) where housing is significantly cheaper
3. Or the return decision deserves serious reconsideration

===

CANADA VS. HOME COUNTRY: THE BREAK-EVEN POINT

For an Indian tech professional:

Canada beats returning home financially when:
- Household income is above CAD $150,000 with dual earners
- Living in a mid-cost Canadian city (not Vancouver/Toronto core)
- The home-country comparison is Tier 2 city (not Mumbai/Bangalore/Hyderabad)

Home country beats Canada financially when (in PPP terms):
- User has senior title at home-country company (₹50L+ in Bangalore)
- Canada savings rate drops below CAD $25,000/year
- User is in single-income family in Vancouver or Toronto core on <CAD $160K

The immigration security premium:
- Canadian PR is worth something the savings model doesn't capture: the eliminated cost of immigration uncertainty, visa renewal, H-1B lottery anxiety, job lock
- Some users rationally stay in Canada at lower savings because the psychological value of "I can choose my job freely and I won't be deported" is real

===

SENSITIVITY ANALYSIS: WHAT MOVES THE NEEDLE MOST

Ranked by impact on annual savings (CAD):
1. Housing cost: Difference between Toronto core vs. Mississauga/Brampton = $600–$900/month = $7,200–$10,800/year
2. Second income: Adding a working partner at CAD $60K adds ~$40,000 net take-home
3. Childcare: CWELCC subsidy vs. unsubsidized daycare: $1,000–$1,800/month delta = $12,000–$21,600/year
4. Province: BC vs. Ontario vs. Alberta: roughly $3,000–$8,000/year difference in provincial tax at comparable incomes
5. Salary: Each $10K CAD increase in gross yields approximately $5,500–$6,500 in additional net after Ontario/BC taxes

The biggest lever a Canadian-based user can pull is: dual income. A single-income family in a high-cost Canadian city has a very difficult savings arithmetic regardless of gross income level.
