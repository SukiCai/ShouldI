THE CORE PRINCIPLE

"Compare savings rate, not gross salary" is the right heuristic but useless without a template. Most people hear this and nod — then continue comparing gross numbers. This file provides the actual calculation framework.

The core insight: A $200K salary in San Francisco that saves $30K/year may lose to a ¥30K/month role in a tier-2 Chinese city that saves ¥20K/month ($2,800) — but the reverse might also be true. The only way to know is to run the numbers.

===

STEP 1: US SCENARIO — NET SAVINGS CALCULATION

Take gross salary and subtract line by line:

GROSS SALARY: $X

Deductions:
(-) Federal income tax: Effective rate ~20–32% depending on income bracket (single or married)
(-) State income tax: 0% (Texas, Florida) to 13.3% (California); varies enormously
(-) FICA (Social Security + Medicare): 7.65% on first ~$168,000, 1.45% above
(-) 401k contribution: Optional but important — reduces taxable income now, is NOT net savings yet (locked until 59½)
(-) Rent: Major variable; $2,500–$5,500/month in major US tech hubs (SF, NYC, Seattle)
(-) Health insurance: $200–$800/month premium out of pocket for individual; more for family
(-) Immigration legal fees: H-1B attorney ($3,000–$8,000/year), green card attorney ($5,000–$15,000 one-time), application fees
(-) Student loan payments: If applicable
(-) Childcare: $1,500–$3,500/month per child in major US cities

RESULT: NET ANNUAL SAVINGS (liquid, spendable, or investable)

Example calculation:
$200K gross in San Francisco:
- Federal tax: ~$47K
- California state tax: ~$18K
- FICA: ~$12K
- Rent (2BR): $48K/year
- Health insurance: $8K
- Immigration legal: $5K
- No children, no student loans

= $200K - $47K - $18K - $12K - $48K - $8K - $5K = **$62K net savings**

Note: The 401k contribution ($23K max) reduces federal tax liability; if contributed fully, shifts the calculation by ~$8K in tax savings. But that money isn't liquid until 59½.

===

STEP 2: HOME COUNTRY SCENARIO — NET SAVINGS CALCULATION

CHINA EXAMPLE (Shanghai tech job):

GROSS SALARY: ¥600,000/year (~$84K at current rates)

Deductions:
(-) Individual Income Tax (IIT): China's IIT is progressive; ¥600K earner pays ~¥120K–¥150K
(-) Social insurance: Employee portion ~7.5–12% depending on city
(-) Housing: Shanghai 2BR apartment ¥8,000–¥18,000/month in good districts
(-) No immigration cost (you're home)
(-) Children: International school ¥200,000–¥350,000/year (if applicable)

= ¥600K - ¥135K (IIT) - ¥60K (social insurance) - ¥120K (housing) = **¥285K net savings** (~$40K)

BUT: In PPP terms, ¥285K in Shanghai buys more than $40K in San Francisco. The domestic purchasing power for food, domestic services, entertainment is roughly 2–3× higher.

Saving ¥285K/year in Shanghai ≈ financial equivalent of $60K–$80K/year in US terms if measured by lifestyle.

===

STEP 3: PPP ADJUSTMENT — CONVERT TO COMPARABLE TERMS

The savings rate comparison must be in comparable units. Options:

Option A: Convert home-country savings to USD at exchange rate (conservative; understates home-country purchasing power)
Option B: Compare savings as % of lifestyle cost (normalize to "months of lifestyle funding")
Option C: Use IMF/World Bank PPP conversion factor

PPP conversion factors (approximate, 2024):
- China: 1 USD in PPP ≈ 4 RMB (meaning ¥40,000 buys roughly what $10,000 buys in the US in domestic goods)
- India: 1 USD in PPP ≈ 26 INR (meaning ₹260,000 buys roughly what $10,000 buys in the US)

Application: If you're saving ¥300,000/year in China, the PPP equivalent is approximately $75,000/year in US purchasing power — even though the exchange rate says only $42,000.

Important caveat: PPP applies to local goods (food, domestic services, rent). It does NOT apply to:
- US-denominated assets (stocks, real estate in the US)
- International travel
- Imported goods (electronics, luxury goods)
- International school fees for children (priced in local but at international rates)

===

STEP 4: WEALTH ACCUMULATION COMPARISON

Beyond annual savings, compare the 10-year and 20-year trajectories:

US trajectory:
- Annual liquid savings: $62K
- 401k contributions: $23K (locked but growing)
- Employer 401k match: ~$5K
- Stock options/RSUs vesting: Variable

Home-country trajectory:
- Annual savings (PPP-adjusted): $65K equivalent
- Local investment returns: Variable (Chinese stock market has had significant volatility; Indian stock market has grown strongly)
- Real estate: Home-country real estate has been strong for some periods, weak for others; Chinese real estate specifically is in a downturn as of 2023–2025

The critical long-term difference: US 401k grows tax-deferred and is in USD. Home-country savings are in local currency with local return dynamics. Currency risk is real — RMB and INR have both experienced significant depreciation vs. USD in certain periods.

===

STEP 5: INCLUDE THE INVISIBLE COSTS

Items that frequently get omitted from the comparison:

STAYING IN US:
- Immigration legal fees: $5,000–$15,000/year in H-1B/green card years
- Premium for job mobility limitation (can't freely switch employers on H-1B without risk)
- Psychological cost of visa uncertainty — hard to quantify but real

RETURNING HOME:
- Tax complexity on return: Consult with cross-border CPA; one-time cost of $3,000–$8,000 for departure year filing, potentially larger if covered expatriate
- Career income gap during first 6–12 months of job search and transition
- International school fees if children involved: $15,000–$50,000/year per child
- Travel to maintain US network / family relationships: $5,000–$15,000/year
- Reverse culture shock adjustment period: Reduced productivity; hard to quantify

THE TEMPLATE: ONE-PAGE COMPARISON

| Line Item | US Scenario | Return Scenario |
|-----------|-------------|-----------------|
| Gross salary (USD) | $X | $Y (USD equiv.) |
| Federal/national income tax | (-) | (-) |
| State/local/regional tax | (-) | (-) |
| Social security / NI equivalent | (-) | (-) |
| Housing | (-) | (-) |
| Healthcare | (-) | (-) |
| Immigration costs | (-) | 0 |
| Children's education | (-) | (-) |
| Return-specific one-time costs | 0 | (-) |
| **Net annual savings (USD equiv.)** | **$A** | **$B** |
| Savings rate (% of gross) | A/X | B/Y |
| PPP-adjusted savings (local purchasing power) | — | $B × PPP factor |
| 10-year projection (compounded at 6%) | A × 13.2 | B × 13.2 |

If B/Y (home country savings rate) ≥ A/X (US savings rate) in PPP terms, the financial case for return is not losing. If it's significantly lower, the US trajectory generates more real wealth despite higher gross comparison numbers.

===

WHEN THE MATH ALONE DOESN'T DECIDE

After running the model, many people find the two options are within 10–20% of each other financially. At that point, the non-financial variables dominate:
- Family proximity
- Reverse culture shock risk
- Career ceiling expectations
- Partner and children
- Sense of home and belonging

The model's purpose is not to produce a definitive answer — it's to remove the illusion that one option is obviously financially dominant when it's not.
