OVERVIEW: H-1B APPROVALS FY2023

Total approved H-1B petitions (FY2023): 266,900
  - Initial approvals: 131,970
  - Continuing approvals (extensions, amendments): 134,930

Cap-exempt approvals (universities, nonprofits): 39,700
Cap-subject new employment approvals: 65,000 (regular cap) + 20,000 (US master's exemption) = 85,000 slots

Average annual approval rate relative to registrations: approximately 22–28% (varies by year and registration volume)

===

NATIONALITY BREAKDOWN (FY2023)

India: 72.3% of all H-1B approvals (approximately 193,000 workers)
China: 11.7% of all H-1B approvals (approximately 31,200 workers)
Canada: 1.4%
South Korea: 1.2%
Philippines: 1.1%
All other countries: 12.3%

Policy implication for Indian nationals: Despite identical per-country green card quotas (roughly 7% of annual EB visas), Indian nationals represent 72% of H-1B workers — creating the mathematically catastrophic EB backlog (see eb_backlog_sourced_data.txt).

===

OCCUPATION BREAKDOWN (FY2023)

Computer-related occupations: 65.1% of all approvals
  - Computer Occupations (SOC 15-xxxx): most common single group
  - Top specific roles: Software developers, software QA engineers, systems analysts, data scientists

Architecture, Engineering, and Surveying: 7.3%
Education, Social Sciences, and Related Occupations: 5.8%
Administrative Specializations (includes management): 5.1%
All other occupations: 16.7%

For PM roles specifically:
- Product managers typically file under SOC 11-3021 (Computer and Information Systems Managers) or 11-2021 (Marketing Managers)
- Computer and Information Systems Managers had median H-1B wage of $155,700 in FY2023

WAGE DISTRIBUTION (FY2023)

Median wage for all H-1B workers: $108,000
Median wage, Computer occupations: $124,000
Median wage, Computer and Information Systems Managers: $155,700
Wage Level distribution (all H-1B):
  - Level I: 10%
  - Level II: 36%
  - Level III: 35%
  - Level IV: 19%

Note: Under the wage-weighted lottery (effective February 27, 2026), Level IV workers receive 4 entries vs. Level I's 1 entry. The current wage distribution shows most H-1B workers are Level II–III, meaning Level IV workers will have disproportionate lottery advantage.

STAY-OR-RETURN IMPLICATION

For Indian nationals specifically:
- 72.3% of all H-1B approvals are Indian nationals
- Same Indians face the EB-2 134-year backlog (see sourced data)
- This creates a structural situation: indefinite H-1B renewal as the de facto trajectory for Indian tech workers who do not return or find alternative paths

The math:
- ~193,000 new/renewed Indian H-1B workers per year
- ~40,000 total EB visas per year across all countries combined
- India's share: approximately 2,800-3,200 visas/year
- At 525,000+ pending petitions for India: 525,000 / 3,000 ≈ 175 years of backlog at current rates

For Chinese nationals:
- 11.7% of H-1B approvals → approximately 31,200 workers
- EB-2 China backlog: 5–10 year wait (manageable)
- EB-3 China backlog: 3–8 year wait
- Qualitatively different stay-or-return calculus than for Indian nationals

===

H-1B PORTABILITY AND JOB CHANGE (AC21)

Under American Competitiveness in the 21st Century Act (AC21):
- An H-1B worker with an approved I-140 (green card petition) and a priority date in queue can change employers after 180 days if:
  1. The new role is in the "same or similar occupational classification"
  2. The I-140 was filed before the priority date became current

Practical implication: For Indian nationals with approved I-140s deep in the EB-2 queue, job changes are possible but require careful AC21 compliance. This creates moderate job lock — not absolute, but real.

Layoff scenario: 60-day grace period to find new employer or change status. Does not reset priority date if I-140 was approved.
