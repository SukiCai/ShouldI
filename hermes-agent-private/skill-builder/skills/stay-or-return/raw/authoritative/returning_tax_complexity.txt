THE CORE PROBLEM: INVISIBLE TAX COSTS IN RETURN DECISIONS

Most people comparing "staying in US vs. returning home" look at gross income, cost of living, and savings rate. They systematically ignore 4–6 specific tax consequences that can cost $50,000–$500,000+ if managed badly. These are the invisible line items.

===

TAX ITEM 1: GREEN CARD ABANDONMENT VS. KEEPING IT "JUST IN CASE"

This is the most common expensive mistake.

If you keep your green card after returning: The IRS treats you as a US tax resident indefinitely, regardless of where you live. This means:
- You must file US taxes every year on worldwide income (including China/India salary)
- You must report all foreign financial accounts (FBAR/Form 8938)
- You must potentially pay US tax on investment gains in home country
- This obligation continues until you formally abandon the green card (Form I-407)

The trap: Many returnees keep the green card "just in case we want to come back." This sounds prudent but creates permanent US tax exposure. The decision to keep or abandon must be made deliberately with a tax advisor, not by default.

If you properly abandon the green card:
- File Form I-407 (Abandonment of Lawful Permanent Resident Status)
- File Form 8854 if you are a "covered expatriate"
- Your US tax residency ends on your "Residency Termination Date"
- FBAR obligation ends after final departure year filing

===

TAX ITEM 2: COVERED EXPATRIATE STATUS AND EXIT TAX

Who triggers exit tax:
- Green card holder for 8+ of the last 15 years (most H-1B→GC holders will qualify)
- Plus either: net worth over $2M, or average annual net US tax liability over $190K for the past 5 years

What the exit tax does:
- Treats you as if you sold all your worldwide assets on the day you expatriate
- Any gain above the exclusion amount (~$869,000 for 2026) is taxed at capital gains rates
- This means unrealized gains in US stocks, 401k, real estate, etc. become taxable events at departure

Most employed professionals with less than $2M net worth and under the income threshold are NOT covered expatriates, meaning exit tax doesn't apply. But verify with a cross-border tax advisor before assuming.

===

TAX ITEM 3: 401(K) AND IRA — WHAT ACTUALLY HAPPENS

Option 1: Leave it in place (most common, usually best)
- Money continues to grow tax-deferred
- Required Minimum Distributions (RMDs) start at age 73 under SECURE 2.0 Act
- US withholds 30% on distributions to non-residents by default
- File Form W-8BEN to claim treaty benefits — US-India tax treaty Article 20 may reduce withholding to 15% or zero for periodic payments
- US-China treaty has similar provisions; verify treaty rate with your custodian

Option 2: Roll over to IRA
- If your employer plan doesn't allow keeping after separation, roll to IRA within 60 days
- IRAs remain under same 30% withholding rules for non-residents unless treaty claimed

Option 3: Early withdrawal (age < 59½)
- 10% IRS penalty + ordinary income tax + state tax
- Almost always the worst option financially
- Only justifiable if you need the cash immediately and have no other source

===

TAX ITEM 4: FBAR (FOREIGN BANK ACCOUNT REPORTING)

Who must file: US persons (citizens, green card holders, H-1B visa holders on a long-term basis) with foreign accounts exceeding $10,000 total at any point in the year.

After returning home: As a non-resident alien (after properly terminating US residency), FBAR obligation typically ends.

Final year filing: File FBAR covering the departure year through your residency termination date.

Penalty for non-compliance (while you were still US resident): Civil penalties up to $10,000/year (non-willful) or 50% of account balance/year (willful). Criminal prosecution possible. This is not theoretical — IRS actively enforces for prior years of non-compliance.

===

TAX ITEM 5: INDIA-SPECIFIC — RNOR STATUS AND COORDINATION

When an Indian national returns to India, they typically qualify as RNOR (Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident) for the first 2 years:
- RNOR is taxed in India only on India-sourced income (not on foreign income)
- This means US retirement account distributions, US investment income, and US-source income during RNOR years may not be taxed in India
- Coordinate timing: Make large US retirement withdrawals during RNOR period to minimize combined US + India tax

After RNOR period ends: Become "Resident and Ordinarily Resident" — then taxed in India on worldwide income, potentially triggering double taxation on US accounts without careful treaty planning.

===

TAX ITEM 6: CHINA-SPECIFIC — NO WORLDWIDE INCOME TAX TREATY BENEFITS

For Chinese returnees:
- China taxes worldwide income for residents who are physically present >183 days/year
- Unlike India (which has a clear RNOR window), China's tax residency rules are stricter
- The US-China tax treaty is complex and has limited exemptions for retirement income
- No equivalent of India's RNOR grace period
- Result: Chinese returnees need to plan US asset structure (timing of liquidation, treaty claims) before returning to avoid paying double taxes

===

PRACTICAL IMPLICATIONS FOR THE STAY-OR-RETURN DECISION

Returning is NOT tax-neutral. A comparison that only looks at salary differences misses:

1. If you keep your green card: Add ongoing US tax compliance costs ($2,000–$5,000/year for CPA fees alone, plus actual tax liability on home-country income)

2. If you return within 8 years of getting green card: No exit tax risk; this is actually the lower-risk timing

3. If you return after 8 years with green card: Get exit tax analysis done before departure

4. 401k strategy: Leaving it in place is usually best; coordinate distributions during RNOR period (India) or before reaching high income years in home country

5. Real estate in US: Selling before departure avoids non-resident withholding rules; keeping requires future FIRPTA compliance (15% withholding on sale proceeds for non-residents)

The total hidden tax cost of a poorly-managed return can be $50,000–$200,000 or more. This needs to be modeled explicitly in any savings-rate comparison between staying and returning.
