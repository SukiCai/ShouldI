SCALE OF THE BACKLOG (AUTHORITATIVE DATA)

Source: USCIS I-140 approved petition data (March 2023):
- EB-2 India: 395,958 approved petitions awaiting visas; 90% (358,078 cases) originated from India
- EB-3 India: 129,754 approved petitions awaiting visas; 89% originated from India
- Total: Over 525,000 approved petitions waiting — nearly all from India

Annual visa supply vs. demand:
- Annual cap: approximately 40,000 employment-based visas available per country (across all EB categories combined)
- Annual demand: over 180,000 applications for India alone
- The quota includes principal applicants AND all dependents — a family of 4 uses 4 visa slots

===

WAIT TIME ESTIMATES (WITH SOURCING)

EB-2 India (as of 2024):
- Priority date cut-off: May 15, 2012 (meaning only people who filed before May 2012 can now apply)
- Cato Institute estimate: 134 years wait time for EB-2 India under current demand and quota assumptions
- Other estimates vary by assumption: some models project 80–150 years depending on demand projections
- Note: All projections are models, not guarantees. Actual wait times depend on annual demand changes, legislative relief, and visa recapture acts. The Cato 134-year figure is widely cited; it represents a projection, not a statutory deadline.

EB-3 India (as of 2024):
- Priority date cut-off: approximately August 2012
- Cato Institute / NFAP estimates: approximately 40 years

Visa exhaustion timing:
- All available EB-3 and EW visas for FY2025 were exhausted by September 9, 2025
- All available EB-3 and EW visas for FY2024 were exhausted by August 16, 2024
- This means even approved petitions go unfilled at end of fiscal year when caps are hit

===

CHINESE NATIONALS: A DIFFERENT PICTURE

China (PRC) nationals also face EB-2 and EB-3 backlogs, but significantly shorter than India's:
- EB-2 China: Typically 5–10 year backlog depending on demand
- EB-3 China: Typically 3–8 year backlog
- Priority dates for China are materially more current than India

Why the difference: Per-country caps are equal (roughly 7% of annual employment-based visas each), but the number of applicants from India vastly exceeds those from China. This makes India's backlog catastrophically longer despite equal policy treatment.

===

WHAT THE BACKLOG MEANS IN PRACTICE

For Indian nationals on H-1B:
- Approved I-140 = you have a spot in the queue but cannot get the green card until your priority date becomes current
- During the wait: must renew H-1B every 1–3 years; job change is restricted (AC21 portability helps but has conditions); travel requires advance parole or valid H-1B stamp
- If laid off: typically 60-day grace period to find new employer or leave the US
- Dependent H-4 spouses: H-4 EAD allows working, but H-4 EAD depends on spouse's I-140 approval and certain H-1B stage

For Indian nationals on F-1/OPT transitioning to H-1B:
- H-1B lottery is the first constraint (now weighted by wage level)
- If selected, the I-140 → green card queue is 50–100+ years
- Effectively means: staying in the US on H-1B for an Indian national means accepting indefinite temporary status

===

HEDGING LANGUAGE FOR PROJECTIONS

The 134-year and 40-year figures should be understood as:
- Based on current demand rates and current annual quota — both can change
- Congress has periodically passed or debated visa recapture acts that could accelerate dates
- Major tech layoffs in 2022–2024 reduced some I-140 filing rates temporarily
- Country-neutral allocation proposals have been debated but not enacted

Using these figures: Present as "estimates suggest 50–100+ years under current policy" rather than as fixed facts. The magnitude is clearly catastrophic; the exact year is a projection artifact.

===

ALTERNATIVE PATHWAYS (REFERENCED)

EB-1A (Extraordinary Ability): No employer sponsorship required, no per-country cap backlog currently for India, requires demonstrating extraordinary ability through publications, awards, salary above peers, etc. Process: ~2–3 years total vs. 134 years for EB-2.

NIW (National Interest Waiver, EB-2): Self-petition, no employer required, same India backlog as EB-2 but avoids PERM labor certification complexity.

EB-5 (Investor Visa): No per-country backlog currently; requires $800K–$1.05M investment in job-creating enterprise; processed in 4–12 months for rural/TEA projects.

O-1A (Extraordinary Ability, non-immigrant): Allows continued US work without H-1B cap or lottery, with no per-country backlog constraints; renewable indefinitely while working toward EB-1A.
