## PGWP Program Scale — 2023 IRCC Data

- **243,294 new PGWP holders** issued in 2023 — up 214% from 74,480 in 2018
- PGWP program has become one of IRCC's largest temporary resident programs
- **75% of PGWP holders** reported T4 employment income in the first year after graduation (Statistics Canada CRA-linked data)
- 25% either unemployed, working informally, or left Canada

---

## Field Alignment Gap: Where PGWP Holders Actually Work

IRCC and Statistics Canada data reveal a persistent gap between PGWP holder education levels and where they find employment:

### Top sectors employing PGWP holders (IRCC, 2023):
1. **Retail Trade** — highest volume, predominantly TEER 4/5 roles
2. **Food Services and Accommodation** — second highest, also predominantly TEER 4/5
3. **Healthcare and Social Assistance** — high volume, mix of TEER 1-3 (nurses, allied health) and TEER 4/5
4. **Professional, Scientific and Technical Services** — growing share, predominantly TEER 0-2
5. **Educational Services** — teaching assistants, instructors, mix of TEER levels

**Critical finding**: A substantial proportion of PGWP holders employed in Retail Trade and Food Services are working in TEER 4/5 occupations that do NOT qualify as skilled work experience for Canadian Experience Class. These graduates may hold open work permits and be legally employed for years while never accumulating CEC-eligible experience.

### What this means for PR pathways:
- PGWP + TEER 4/5 employment = cannot directly access CEC after 1 year
- Must transition to TEER 0-3 role before CEC clock starts
- Some PNP streams (e.g., Ontario OINP Masters Graduate stream) don't require skilled work experience — valuable workaround for graduates without TEER 0-3 employment

## PR Conversion Rates from PGWP

IRCC longitudinal tracking data (cohort analysis):

- Of PGWP holders who arrived 2015-2018: approximately **35-40%** obtained permanent residence within 5 years
- Among those who obtained PR: **Canadian Experience Class** was the most common pathway (~55%), followed by PNP (~25%) and Federal Skilled Worker (~15%)
- PGWP holders with Canadian master's degrees: higher PR conversion rates due to education points in CRS + 3-year PGWP providing more time

**By education level (CRS education points)**:
- Master's degree: 135 points (with Canadian education points: 119 CRS base + additional from language)
- Bachelor's degree: 120 points
- PhD: 140 points
- Two or more post-secondary degrees: up to 128 points

## Language as the Primary CRS Variable

Research from CIC News analysis of Express Entry draws (2023-2025):

- **French language proficiency** remains the single most impactful CRS optimization for most PGWP holders
- French CLB 7+ on TEF Canada / TCF Canada adds ~25 points to CRS (Bilingual Bonus)
- French CLB 9+ on writing/speaking adds up to 50 additional points
- IRCC prioritizes French-speaking immigrants and runs dedicated "French-language proficiency" Express Entry draws with lower cut-offs

**Recent Express Entry cut-off scores** (IRCC 2024-2025 draws):
- General draws (CEC + FSW): typically 480-520 range
- French-language proficiency draws: typically 365-400 range (significantly lower)
- Healthcare occupations draws: 430-460
- STEM occupations draws: 481-500

**Implication**: A PGWP holder who achieves French CLB 7 can potentially qualify for an ITA at scores 80-120 points below the general draw — equivalent to several years of additional Canadian work experience.

## Provincial Variation in PGWP Employment Outcomes

Conference Board of Canada data shows significant regional variation:

### Toronto/Ontario:
- Largest PGWP holder population; highest competition
- OINP Masters Graduate stream offers direct PNP path without job offer
- IT and finance sectors hire PGWP holders at competitive rates
- Cost of living highest in Canada; stipends/salaries sometimes insufficient

### Vancouver/British Columbia:
- Strong tech sector via BC PNP Tech Pilot
- BC PNP Tech: targeted draws for workers in specific NOC codes, no minimum CRS
- Processing: 2-4 months for BC PNP nomination → 6-month federal processing
- Higher cost of living; housing market extremely competitive

### Montreal/Quebec:
- Quebec operates independently — Quebec Selection Certificate (CSQ) instead of Express Entry
- PGWP holders in Quebec must apply through Quebec's immigration system (QSWP or PRTQ)
- **Key advantage**: Montreal has significantly lower cost of living than Toronto/Vancouver
- **Language requirement**: French proficiency is essentially required for Quebec immigration — DELF/DALF or TEF Canada scores needed for CSQ
- Large tech ecosystem (Ubisoft, CAE, BRP, Element AI) but heavily francophone

### Smaller provinces (Saskatchewan, Manitoba, Nova Scotia):
- Lower CRS cut-offs for provincial nomination draws
- Less competition than Ontario/BC
- PNP streams specifically designed for international graduates of provincial universities
- Salaries lower but also lower cost of living
- Saskatchewan Immigrant Nominee Program (SINP): pathways for graduates of Saskatchewan institutions with job offers

## Employer Perceptions of International Graduates

Research from Institute for Canadian Citizenship (2023 survey of 500+ Canadian employers):

- **68%** of employers said they would hire an international graduate on PGWP without concern
- **22%** expressed uncertainty about open work permit rules (many thought they needed to "sponsor" even for PGWP holders)
- **10%** said they preferred domestic candidates for immigration compliance reasons
- Employers in tech/finance: highest comfort with PGWP holders
- Employers in healthcare/government: more procedural requirements but not hostile

**Key finding**: The "no sponsorship" barrier for PGWP holders is not primarily employer hostility — it's employer misunderstanding. Many employers who say "we don't sponsor" don't realize that PGWP holders don't need sponsorship. Proactively explaining: "I hold an open work permit — no employer action required" resolves most objections.

## Salary Outcomes for PGWP Holders vs. Domestic Graduates

Statistics Canada data shows persistent wage gap:

- International graduates earn **10-25% less** than comparable domestic graduates in first employment in Canada
- Gap narrows significantly with 2-3 years Canadian experience
- Gap is smallest in STEM fields (tech, engineering): ~8-12% initial gap, closes within 2-3 years
- Gap is largest in finance and business: 15-25% initial gap, takes longer to close
- PR holders earn roughly equivalent to domestic graduates after 3-5 years

**Interpretation**: The initial wage gap reflects the discount many PGWP holders accept to get into the Canadian labour market and accumulate CEC-eligible experience. It is not permanent but is real — students should plan for it and not over-leverage on cost-of-living assumptions.
