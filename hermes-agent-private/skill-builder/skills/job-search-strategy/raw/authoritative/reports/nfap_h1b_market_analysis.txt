## The Scale of the H-1B Supply-Demand Gap

The National Foundation for American Policy (NFAP) is the leading non-partisan research organization tracking H-1B visa data.

### FY2025 Registration Data (NFAP Analysis):
- **442,000 unique beneficiaries** entered the H-1B registration in FY2025
- **Annual H-1B cap**: 85,000
- **Ratio**: 5.2 applications for every available slot
- **Estimated rejected qualified applicants**: 300,000 to 350,000 high-skilled foreign nationals blocked annually from US employment due to cap limitations

"The 85,000 annual limit on new H-1B petitions for high-skilled foreign nationals is low, equaling 0.05% of the U.S. labor force, and remains the leading immigration problem for most tech companies."
— NFAP, November 2025

### Employer Distribution (FY2025):
- **28,277 unique US employers** had at least one new H-1B petition approved
- **70% of new H-1B petitions** went to employers that filed 100 or fewer applications — i.e., the majority of H-1B sponsorship comes from mid-sized companies, not just tech giants
- Top 4 sponsors (Amazon, Meta, Microsoft, Google) still represent a disproportionate share of high-volume approvals

**Implication**: The commonly held belief that "only big tech sponsors H-1B" is false. The majority of H-1B positions are at smaller companies. The challenge is identifying which smaller companies have the compliance infrastructure and willingness to sponsor.

## H-1B Is Increasingly Restrictive (NFAP March 2025 Brief)

NFAP documented a consistent tightening trend:

| Metric | FY2019 | FY2022 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|--------|--------|--------|--------|--------|
| Overall denial rate | 24% | 4% | 4.9% | 5.8% |
| RFE rate | 60%+ | ~17% | 21.4% | 23% |

**Context**: The spike in FY2019 (24% denial rate) was driven by an aggressive USCIS policy that was later reversed in courts. The current 5.8% denial rate is near historic lows for straightforward petitions — but the RFE rate creep indicates increasing scrutiny even when petitions are ultimately approved.

## Structural Barrier: The 85,000 Cap Has Not Changed Since 2004

The current H-1B annual cap was set by the H-1B Visa Reform Act of 2004. Since then:
- US GDP has grown approximately 130%
- US tech industry employment has roughly doubled
- International STEM graduate enrollment has increased significantly
- The cap has not changed

"The H-1B is the most restrictive high-skilled work visa in the developed world relative to the size of the labor force and economy it serves." — NFAP Policy Brief, March 2025

### Comparison to other countries (NFAP):
- Canada: No cap on skilled worker admissions through Express Entry
- UK: No cap on Tier 2 (Skilled Worker) visas for shortage occupations
- Germany: Blue Card has effectively no cap for qualified professionals
- Australia: Skills in demand pathway has flexible caps

**Decision implication for job seekers**: Choosing to stay in the US long-term requires surviving the H-1B lottery (35% annual odds for new grads) plus multi-year wait for green card (5-15+ years for most countries; 50+ years for India/China backlog). Canadian permanent residence for the same profile: 18-30 months.

## H-1B Sponsorship Cost for Employers (Why Many Don't Sponsor)

Employers bear significant costs for each H-1B petition:
- **USCIS filing fees**: $730–$4,730 depending on company size and petition type
- **Attorney fees**: $2,000–$6,000+ per petition
- **Premium processing** (optional, speeds decision to 15 business days): Additional $2,805
- **Anti-fraud fee**: $500 (first-time petitioners)
- **ACWIA training fee**: $750–$1,500 depending on company size
- **Total typical cost**: $5,000–$15,000+ per sponsorship

**This explains small company reluctance**: For a 30-person startup, $10,000 in immigration costs per international hire is a real budget consideration. For Amazon, it's negligible.

**Job seeker strategy implication**: When a small company says "we don't sponsor," often the real barrier is legal cost and compliance uncertainty, not policy opposition. Some job seekers have negotiated a "sponsorship assistance" clause where they contribute to or pre-pay attorney costs — though this is legally complicated and requires immigration attorney review.

## Cap-Exempt Pathways: Avoiding the Lottery

NFAP and American Immigration Council identify key cap-exempt employer categories:
1. **Universities** (all levels, including community colleges and online universities)
2. **Non-profit research organizations** affiliated with universities
3. **Government research organizations** (NIH, NASA, national labs)
4. **Non-profit entities engaged in research** in the public interest

**Practical implication**: An international graduate who takes a research associate or staff scientist role at a university lab avoids the H-1B lottery entirely. This is a legitimate career strategy, not just a stopgap — many research roles at major universities are well-compensated and career-building.

**Example (Farah, AI researcher, University of Illinois)**: Transitioned into a research associate position under the university's cap-exempt H-1B framework after completing STEM OPT, avoiding the lottery entirely.

## The H-1B Transfer (Portability) Provision

Once an H-1B petition has been APPROVED (not just filed), the worker can:
- Change employers without waiting for a new approval ("H-1B portability" under AC21)
- New employer files a new petition, but worker can start immediately upon filing
- No need to wait for approval, and does NOT count against the cap

**Why this matters**: H-1B workers are NOT as "locked in" to their initial employer as commonly believed. After first H-1B approval, switching jobs is administratively manageable. The "body shop" trap is mainly a first-petition risk — once you have an H-1B stamp and approval notice, your options expand significantly.

## Median Salary Data for H-1B Workers (DOL LCA Data)

From DOL H-1B Labor Condition Application public disclosure data:
- **Software Engineer Level II**: $130,000–$160,000 median (varies by metro area)
- **Data Scientist Level II**: $120,000–$155,000 median
- **Software Engineer Level I** (entry): $95,000–$130,000 median
- **Financial Analyst**: $85,000–$120,000 median
- **Mechanical Engineer**: $80,000–$110,000 median

**Geographic variation**: San Francisco Bay Area LCA wages run 30–50% above the national H-1B wage. New York 20–30% above. Austin/Seattle/Boston 10–20% above. This data is publicly searchable at H1BData.info.
