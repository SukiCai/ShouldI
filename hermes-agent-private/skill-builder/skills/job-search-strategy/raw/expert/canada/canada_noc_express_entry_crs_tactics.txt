## The Real Strategic Framework for PGWP Holders

The job search for a PGWP holder has two phases with different objectives:

**Phase 1 (Year 1 on PGWP)**: Find employment in NOC TEER 0/1/2/3. Any qualifying employment starts the CEC clock. Priority: get into a skilled role, not necessarily the dream role.

**Phase 2 (Year 2-3 on PGWP)**: Accumulate 12 months of TEER 0/1/2/3 experience → apply to Express Entry → optimize CRS score → receive ITA → PR application.

Most PGWP holders underestimate Phase 1. They job search like domestic graduates (targeting their ideal role) and spend 6-12 months unemployed or in TEER 4/5 work. This compresses or eliminates the CEC window.

**Rule of thumb**: With a 3-year PGWP, you have 18-24 months to get into a TEER 0/1/2/3 role and accumulate 12 months before needing to file for CEC. 24 months of timeline gone → you're relying on renewal, alternative streams, or leaving Canada.

## CRS Score Optimization — Priority Order (Post-March 2025)

With LMIA job offer points removed, the remaining optimization levers in order of impact:

### 1. Provincial Nomination (+600 CRS points → guarantees ITA)
The single highest-impact move. Any PNP nomination adds 600 points — functionally guaranteeing an Invitation to Apply.

**Most accessible PNP streams for international graduates:**
- **Ontario OINP Masters Graduate Stream**: No job offer required. Must have Ontario master's degree. Direct nomination path. Apply as soon as you graduate. Processing ~6-12 months.
- **BC PNP Tech Pilot (Skills Immigration)**: Must have job offer in target NOC code. BC does targeted draws; no minimum CRS required. Processing: 2-4 months for nomination.
- **Alberta AAIP — Graduate Entrepreneur Stream**: For graduates starting businesses in Alberta.
- **Saskatchewan SINP — International Graduate Category**: For graduates of Saskatchewan post-secondary institutions with job offer. Lower CRS cut-offs.

**Strategic implication**: If you're deciding between Ontario and BC for job search, Ontario OINP Masters Graduate stream (no job offer required) may be more accessible than BC PNP (requires job offer). However, BC tech salaries are often higher.

### 2. French Language Proficiency (+25 to +100 CRS points)
Still the most accessible non-PNP optimization post-LMIA removal.

**CRS bonus breakdown:**
- First official language (English) CLB 9+ in all skills: base score
- Second official language (French) CLB 7+ in any skill: +25 CRS points
- Second official language (French) CLB 7+ in all four skills: up to +50 CRS points
- "Francophone" draw eligibility: French CLB 7+ with English CLB 4+ → separate dedicated draws at cut-offs ~365-400 vs. general ~500

**Tests accepted:**
- TEF Canada (Test d'évaluation de français) — IRCC approved
- TCF Canada (Test de connaissance du français) — IRCC approved
- DELF/DALF does NOT count for Express Entry (only for Quebec)

**ROI calculation**: 6 months of French study + TEF Canada exam can add 25-50 CRS points. At current cut-off levels, this can be the difference between waiting 18 months and waiting 6 months for an ITA.

### 3. Canadian Work Experience (+15 to +80 CRS points)
Each additional year of skilled Canadian work experience adds CRS points.

| Canadian skilled work experience | CRS points (single factor) |
|----------------------------------|---------------------------|
| 1 year | +40 points |
| 2 years | +53 points |
| 3 years | +64 points |
| 4 years | +72 points |
| 5+ years | +80 points |

**Implication**: PGWP holders should enter the Express Entry pool after 1 year of skilled work, but those who can wait until 2 years gain ~13 additional CRS points. Calculate the trade-off against PGWP expiry.

### 4. IELTS/CELPIP Score Optimization (+0 to +60 CRS points)
Most CEC-eligible PGWP holders already score CLB 9+ in English. Increasing from CLB 9 to CLB 10 adds only a few points. Focus effort on French (lever #2) before over-investing in English.

### 5. Canadian Education Bonus (+15 CRS points)
If you completed at least 2 academic years of full-time study at a Canadian post-secondary, you receive a +15 CRS bonus applied to the education category. Most master's students qualify automatically.

## Key Differences Between Federal and Quebec Immigration

**Federal Express Entry (for Ontario, BC, Alberta, SK, NS, etc.):**
- IRCC manages; CRS score determines ITA
- CEC: 1 year skilled work in Canada
- Language: English (CLB 7 minimum) or French
- Processing after ITA: ~6 months for PR

**Quebec immigration (for Montreal/Quebec City):**
- Quebec selects its own immigrants
- Quebec Skilled Worker Program (QSWP) or PRTQ (temporary resident transition)
- French is essentially mandatory (strongly weighted)
- Quebec grants CSQ (Quebec Selection Certificate); then federal PR
- Quebec graduates: apply to PRTQ if employed in Quebec on PGWP → faster track

**Strategic choice**: If you have no French and want a clear fast path, focus on Ontario/BC where English-only Express Entry works. If you're willing to invest in French or are in Quebec already, Montreal + PRTQ/QSWP can be faster.

## Networking Strategy Specific to Canada

Canadian hiring is more relationship-driven than US hiring, and less formalized than either UK or Australia:

**Key platforms:**
- **LinkedIn**: Standard everywhere in Canada; cold connection requests with personalized notes work well
- **Eventbrite professional events**: Canadian cities have active industry meetups; immigration-adjacent meetups (Newcomer Fintech Network, ACCES Employment programs) are legitimate networking opportunities
- **ACCES Employment**: Free employment programs specifically for internationally trained professionals. Resume workshops, mentoring, employer connections. Available in GTA, Ottawa, Edmonton.
- **immigrant-specific programs**: Many large Canadian employers (RBC, TD, Deloitte, KPMG) run formal diversity hiring programs for recent international graduates

**Cold outreach in Canada:**
- Canadian professionals are generally responsive to LinkedIn cold messages if they are personalized
- Referencing shared university/program background is effective — Canadian alumni networks are active
- Timing: reach out in September-October and January-February (hiring cycles in Canada)
- Title target: hiring manager or senior individual contributor, not HR recruiter (same as US)

**Government job boards (often overlooked):**
- **Job Bank Canada** (canada.ca/en/services/jobs/opportunities): Federal official job board; government positions; many explicitly say "open to PGWP holders"
- **Immigrant Employment Council**: Province-specific employer networks
- **Magnet (magnet.today)**: Platform specifically connecting newcomers to employers — explicitly filters for immigration status

## Salary Negotiation in Canada

Unlike US (where H-1B LCA wage levels create a negotiation floor), Canadian employers don't have the same prevailing wage infrastructure for PGWP holders.

**Salary benchmarks by field (2024-2025, major Canadian cities):**
- Software engineer (NOC 21211), Toronto: $85,000-$130,000 CAD
- Software engineer, Vancouver: $90,000-$140,000 CAD
- Software engineer, Montreal: $75,000-$115,000 CAD
- Financial analyst (NOC 11102), Toronto: $65,000-$95,000 CAD
- Civil engineer (NOC 21300), Calgary: $75,000-$110,000 CAD
- Registered nurse (NOC 31301), Toronto: $70,000-$90,000 CAD

**Sources for salary research**: Glassdoor Canada, LinkedIn Salary, Levels.fyi (tech only), Payscale Canada.

**Key negotiation difference from US**: In Canada, you can negotiate freely as a PGWP holder — there is no LCA wage level locking you into a specific tier. Negotiate based on market rates. Do not accept below-market offers because of visa insecurity — your PGWP is not employer-specific.
