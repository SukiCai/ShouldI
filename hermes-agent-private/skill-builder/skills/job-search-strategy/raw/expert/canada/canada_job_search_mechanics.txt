## Why Canada Job Search Strategy Differs from US

The fundamental difference: in Canada, employment authorization after graduation is an open work permit (Post-Graduation Work Permit / PGWP) rather than an employer-specific visa. This changes the job search dynamic significantly.

In the US: every employer must be willing to sponsor a specific visa for a specific person → sponsorship is a filter
In Canada: PGWP holders can work for any employer in any role → sponsorship is not a filter for the first 1-3 years

This means Canadian job search strategy for recent international graduates is primarily a job search problem, not a visa problem. The immigration complexity comes later — in converting Canadian work experience into permanent residence.

## Post-Graduation Work Permit (PGWP)

### What it is
An open work permit issued by IRCC to international graduates of eligible Canadian Designated Learning Institutions (DLIs). It allows work for any employer in Canada.

### Duration
- Program 8 months to 2 years: PGWP duration = program length
- Program 2+ years: PGWP duration = 3 years (maximum)
- Two programs combined (if eligible): up to 3 years

**Critical implication for job search**: A 2-year master's degree → 3-year PGWP. A 1-year master's degree → 1-year PGWP. The difference between a 1-year and 2-year program is the difference between 1 year and 3 years of open work authorization. This is a major factor in program selection for international students.

### PGWP does not require employer sponsorship
You apply for PGWP after graduation. Employers do not need to know about your PGWP status before hiring — it's your personal work authorization. You can say: "I have a 3-year open work permit — no sponsorship required."

### What to tell employers
"I have a Post-Graduation Work Permit valid until [date]. No sponsorship is required for this period. After [date], I plan to apply for permanent residence through Express Entry, for which I will be eligible based on my Canadian work experience."

This is a materially different conversation from "I need H-1B sponsorship."

## Labour Market Impact Assessment (LMIA)

### What it is
An LMIA is a document from Employment and Social Development Canada (ESDC) that allows a Canadian employer to hire a foreign worker when no Canadian citizen or permanent resident is available for the role.

LMIA is the Canadian rough equivalent of H-1B sponsorship — it's what you need after your PGWP expires if you haven't obtained PR.

### When PGWP holders need LMIA
Only after PGWP expires. Most PGWP holders should have PR before their PGWP expires if they planned correctly:
- 3-year PGWP → 1 year of work → eligible for Canadian Experience Class → Express Entry draw → PR application → PR typically in 6-12 months = PR before PGWP expires in most cases

### LMIA-exempt work permit categories (important for job search)
Several work permit categories don't require LMIA:

1. **CUSMA (Canada-United States-Mexico Agreement) — TN Visa equivalent**: For US and Mexican citizens only. Specific professional occupations. No numerical cap, no lottery. Much faster than H-1B.

2. **Intracompany Transfers (ICT)**: If you work for a multinational with a Canadian and US/international office, intracompany transfer between countries is possible. Useful for people already working in US wanting to move to Canada or vice versa.

3. **International Agreements**: Various bilateral agreements create LMIA-exempt categories.

4. **Global Talent Stream**: See below — fast-track for tech workers.

## Global Talent Stream (GTS)

### What it is
A stream of the Temporary Foreign Worker Program designed to allow Canadian employers to hire highly skilled workers quickly. Processing time: 2 weeks.

### Two categories:
**Category A**: Employer referred by a designated partner organization (tech industry groups, economic development organizations). Can hire workers in any role.

**Category B**: Hiring for roles on the Global Talent Occupations List — specific tech roles (software engineers, ML engineers, data scientists, etc.) — employer doesn't need referral.

### Why it matters for job search
For tech workers whose PGWP has expired:
- Normal LMIA processing: 3–6 months
- Global Talent Stream: 2 weeks
- For employers, GTS is dramatically faster → tech employers in Canada are much more willing to sponsor tech talent via GTS than US employers are willing to do H-1B

For job seekers who need post-PGWP authorization: "If I need work permit renewal, the Global Talent Stream would apply to my role and takes 2 weeks for your company — it's not a significant burden."

## Express Entry and Canadian Experience Class (CEC)

### The core pathway for PGWP holders

After 1 year of skilled work in Canada on a PGWP:
1. You are eligible for Canadian Experience Class (CEC)
2. CEC is one of the programs in the Express Entry pool
3. CRS (Comprehensive Ranking System) score determines who gets invited
4. CRS score depends on: age, education, language scores (IELTS/CELPIP), Canadian work experience, job offer, provincial nomination

### CRS score factors job seekers can influence:
- **Language scores**: IELTS Academic or CELPIP. Strong English + French → significant bonus (French TEF/TCF). French proficiency can add 25-50 points to CRS score.
- **Job offer**: LMIA-backed job offer adds 50-200 points depending on NOC skill level
- **Provincial Nomination**: Some PNPs have specific tech streams that add 600 points (essentially guaranteeing an ITA)

### Implication for employer conversations:
"After 1 year of working here, I'll be eligible for permanent residence through Canadian Experience Class. This is a very clear pathway — I expect to have PR within 2-3 years of starting." This is a much more credible retention story than the H-1B lottery narrative.

## Provincial Nominee Programs (PNPs)

Many provinces have tech-specific streams that are faster and less competitive than federal Express Entry:

**BC Tech Pilot**: British Columbia invites workers in specific tech NOC codes to apply. No minimum CRS score required — province selects directly.

**Ontario Immigrant Nominee Program (OINP) — Masters Graduate stream**: Ontario graduates from eligible Ontario universities can apply. No job offer required. Quick processing for international STEM graduates.

**Alberta Advantage Immigration Program (AAIP)**: Alberta pathways for workers with job offers or specific credentials.

**Saskatchewan Immigrant Nominee Program (SINP)**: Various occupation-specific streams with lower competition than Ontario/BC.

### Strategic advice:
If your long-term goal is Canadian PR, consider targeting provinces with tech-specific PNP streams and matching your job search to companies in those provinces. A job offer in a PNP-eligible role in Saskatchewan may be a faster PR path than a job offer in Ontario, even if the Ontario offer pays more.

## Canada Job Search: Key Tactical Differences from US

| Dimension | US (OPT/H-1B) | Canada (PGWP) |
|-----------|----------------|----------------|
| Sponsorship filter | Hard filter — must verify upfront | Not needed during PGWP — open permit |
| Employer hesitation | "We don't sponsor" blocks you | Rare during PGWP period |
| Timeline pressure | H-1B lottery = annual binary event | Express Entry = rolling, CRS-based |
| Small employer risk | Very high (immigration attorney needed) | Low during PGWP; medium post-PGWP |
| Disclosure framing | "I need sponsorship" | "I have open work authorization" |
| Networking strategy | Sponsor-friendly company filter first | Standard job search — no filter needed |

### What this means in practice:
Canadian job search for PGWP holders is closer to a domestic job search than to the US international student experience. The primary strategies (network, referrals, cold outreach, LinkedIn) apply without the sponsor-filter overlay.

The immigration complexity for Canada comes AFTER getting the job — in understanding how to maximize CRS scores, time PR application, and leverage PNPs.

## Red Flags Specific to Canadian Market

**Unpaid internships offered to "get Canadian experience"**: Technically illegal for most workers in Canada (Employment Standards Act protections). Employers who offer unpaid internships to international graduates are exploiting visa status.

**"LMIA pending" offers**: Some employers say they'll hire you subject to LMIA approval. LMIA is employer's responsibility and cost — you should never pay for it. Normal processing takes months and can be denied. Be cautious unless you know the employer has successfully done it before.

**Misclassification as contractor**: Some employers hire international workers as independent contractors to avoid immigration compliance. This can affect PR applications (CEC requires employed work, not self-employment).

**Body shop / consulting firm offer with promised LMIA**: Similar to US consulting body shop problem. Verify the company has successfully sponsored workers before.
