## FY2025 H-1B Overall Statistics

- **Total H-1B petitions adjudicated**: 415,275
- **Approved**: 406,349
- **Denied**: 8,926
- **Overall approval rate**: approximately 97.9% (initial petitions) / 88.2% (including continuations)
- **Overall denial rate**: 5.8% (up from 4.9% in FY2024)
- **RFE (Request for Evidence) rate**: 23% — nearly 1 in 4 petitions received additional scrutiny before decision (up from 21.4% in FY2024)
- **Different employers approved**: 28,277 unique US employers were approved to hire at least one new H-1B worker in FY2025

Source: USCIS official data / H1B Data Hub analysis of USCIS public disclosure data

## Key Risk Factors Identified in USCIS Data

**Third-party placement = highest scrutiny**: The single biggest red flag in USCIS adjudications is when the H-1B worker will be placed at a third-party client site rather than working at the sponsoring employer's own location. This affects all IT staffing and body-shop consulting arrangements.

**Wage Level I filings**: Level 1 filings (entry-level wage tier) face RFE rates 5x higher than Level 4 filings (34.2% vs. 6.8%). USCIS scrutinizes lower-wage positions as potentially not qualifying as "specialty occupations."

**Trend: USCIS is tightening**: Consistent year-over-year deterioration — more RFEs, higher denial rates, more petitions flagged for scrutiny. This trend continued through FY2025.

## DOL Prevailing Wage Requirements

The H-1B employer must pay whichever is higher:
1. **Prevailing wage**: average wage paid to similarly employed workers in the occupation in the same geographic area
2. **Actual wage**: wage paid by the employer to other workers with similar skills/qualifications

**Wage levels** (DOL OFLC / Bureau of Labor Statistics):
- **Level I** (~17th percentile): Entry-level, limited experience
- **Level II** (midpoint of I and IV): Some experience, limited complexity
- **Level III** (higher midpoint): Experienced, complex duties
- **Level IV** (~67th percentile): Fully competent, independent judgment

**Why this matters for salary negotiation**: An employer cannot legally pay an H-1B worker below the prevailing wage for their level. This creates a floor that cannot be negotiated down. However, it also means negotiating UP from Level I to Level II or III can be legitimate (and the employer must pay accordingly to comply).

## H-1B Annual Cap and Lottery (Official Structure)

- **Annual cap**: 85,000 new H-1B petitions per year (20,000 of which are reserved for US advanced degree holders — "Master's cap")
- **FY2025 registrations**: 442,000 unique beneficiaries entered — 5x the available cap
- **USCIS blocked**: 300,000–350,000 qualified applicants annually due to cap limitations
- **Selection rate**: ~35.3% overall (FY2026 data)

**Cap-exempt employers** (not subject to the 85,000 limit):
- Universities and affiliated research institutions
- Government research organizations
- Nonprofit research organizations

**H-1B lottery registration window**: Typically March 1–18 each year. Employer must file registration; selected registrants then file full petition.

**Important for job search timing**: To be selected in H-1B lottery for October 1 start date, employer must register you by March. This means offer + processing needed by February at the latest.

## What to Verify When Evaluating an Employer as H-1B Sponsor

Official resources to check before accepting an offer:
1. **USCIS H-1B Employer Data Hub** (`uscis.gov/tools/reports-and-studies/h-1b-employer-data-hub`): Search any employer to see their petition counts and approval history
2. **DOL OFLC Disclosure Data** (`dol.gov/agencies/eta/foreign-labor/performance`): Verify prevailing wages for your specific occupation and location
3. **E-Verify enrollment**: Employer must be E-Verify enrolled to sponsor STEM OPT; this is a separate but related requirement
4. **H1BData.info** (public LCA database): See what wages the employer has certified for similar positions historically
