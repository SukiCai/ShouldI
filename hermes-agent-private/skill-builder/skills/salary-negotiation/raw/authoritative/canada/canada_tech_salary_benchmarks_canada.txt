WHY CANADIAN SALARY DATA IS NEEDED SEPARATELY

Salary negotiation advice calibrated to US market data (Glassdoor US, levels.fyi US) will mislead Canadian job seekers. The benchmarks are different by 25–40% in USD terms, and the compensation structure is different (less equity, more base-heavy). Using US data to negotiate in Canada results in either:
- Anchoring too high (losing offers by seeming out of touch with Canadian market)
- Anchoring too low (leaving money on the table if you don't know the Canadian ceiling)

Canadian-specific benchmarks are essential for anyone negotiating in the Canadian tech labor market.

GEOGRAPHIC SALARY DIFFERENCES WITHIN CANADA

Highest-paying Canadian tech markets:
1. Toronto (ON): Benchmark city; highest absolute salaries; highest cost of living
2. Vancouver (BC): Within 5–10% of Toronto; second-largest tech hub
3. Ottawa (ON): Shopify HQ; government tech; slightly lower market but growing
4. Waterloo (ON): University corridor; startup dense; 5–15% below Toronto levels

Lower-paying but growing markets:
4. Calgary (AB): Growing tech scene; 10–20% below Toronto for comparable roles; NO provincial income tax (Alberta has no provincial income tax)
5. Edmonton (AB): Similar to Calgary; less tech density
6. Montreal (QC): Bilingual requirements limit some roles; 15–25% below Toronto; lower cost of living partially offsets

Alberta note: The absence of provincial income tax in Alberta means take-home is approximately 8–12% higher than Ontario or BC for the same gross salary. A CAD $130,000 gross in Calgary yields significantly more take-home than the same gross in Toronto or Vancouver.

CANADIAN SALARY NEGOTIATION CONTEXT

Offer structure norms in Canada:
- Most Canadian employers provide an offer with base salary only, benefits overview, and RRSP matching detail
- Equity (RSUs or options) disclosed separately if applicable
- No signing bonus is common but expected to be asked for at tech companies
- Annual review cycles typically 12 months; performance adjustments 2–8% for strong performers

Negotiating salary in Canada vs. US:
- Canadian offers tend to have narrower negotiating bands (3–10% typical vs. US 10–20%)
- Employers are more likely to say "the band is set" and mean it, especially at established companies
- Signing bonus is the most negotiable component at most Canadian employers
- BC pay transparency (all employers from Jan 2026) and Ontario (25+ employees, Jan 2026): You can see the range in the posting

Robert Half survey note: Robert Half Canada surveys are conducted annually across Canadian employers. The salary ranges represent base salaries for permanent positions; they do not include bonuses or equity. They are used as reference by many HR departments to set initial offer ranges.

Statistics Canada SEPH data is based on payroll records and tends to undercount tech compensation relative to direct surveys. Used here for occupational trend analysis; Robert Half and Glassdoor CA are more accurate for individual negotiation benchmarks.
