STATUS: CONFIRMED FINAL RULE (as of December 2025)

The H-1B wage-weighted lottery is NO LONGER a proposed rule — it was finalized.

Key dates:
- Final rule published: December 23, 2025 (Federal Register document 2025-23853)
- Effective date: February 27, 2026
- First lottery under new system: FY2027 cap-subject lottery (registration in early March 2026)

This is now settled law, not a pending proposal. Any guidance referencing it as "proposed" or "pending" is outdated as of early 2026.

===

HOW THE WEIGHTED SYSTEM WORKS

Previous system: Random lottery — every registered H-1B beneficiary had an equal chance regardless of salary or skill level.

New system: Multiple lottery entries based on wage level:
- Wage Level I (entry level): 1 lottery entry
- Wage Level II (qualified workers): 2 lottery entries
- Wage Level III (experienced workers): 3 lottery entries
- Wage Level IV (fully competent): 4 lottery entries

Wage levels are defined by the Department of Labor Prevailing Wage system, based on the Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics (OEWS) survey for the offered occupation and location.

===

SELECTION PROBABILITY CHANGES

Based on National Foundation for American Policy (NFAP) analysis:
- Wage Level IV applicants: probability increases by 107% (more than doubles)
- Wage Level III applicants: probability increases by 55%
- Wage Level II applicants: probability increases by 3%
- Wage Level I applicants: probability falls by 48%

In practical terms: A Level IV tech worker (think senior engineer or principal PM at a major tech company) is now roughly twice as likely to be selected as under the old random system. A Level I worker has approximately half the odds.

===

WHAT THIS MEANS FOR SALARY NEGOTIATION AND CAREER STRATEGY

FOR H-1B APPLICANTS:
The wage-weighted system structurally aligns salary negotiation with lottery success probability. Negotiating a higher salary is no longer just about comp — it directly improves your H-1B selection odds.

Specifically:
- If an offer is at Wage Level II and you can negotiate it to Wage Level III (often $15,000–$30,000 higher), you go from 2 lottery entries to 3 — a 50% increase in selection probability
- The marginal value of each salary dollar is higher for H-1B candidates than for US citizens, because it buys both purchasing power AND immigration security

HOW WAGE LEVELS ARE DETERMINED:
- Set by DOL prevailing wage determinations for the specific SOC code (occupation) and geographic area
- Your employer's attorney calculates which wage level your role qualifies for
- The wage level in the LCA (Labor Condition Application) determines your lottery entries
- You cannot unilaterally choose your wage level — it's based on the job's duties, not just the pay rate

NEGOTIATION IMPLICATION:
When negotiating salary as an H-1B candidate:
- Ask your employer's immigration attorney (or research independently) what salary thresholds separate Wage Level II from III and III from IV for your specific occupation code and location
- Use this information to frame a salary request: "To be at Wage Level IV, my salary needs to be at least [X] for this occupation in this location"
- This argument is independent of market data — it ties the salary request to a structural immigration benefit

===

THE RETENTION VALUE ARGUMENT UPDATED

Prior guidance in salary negotiation resources notes the retention value of H-1B sponsorship: employers who sponsor a visa invest $3,000–$8,000 in immigration fees and attorney costs, making the employee harder to replace.

Under the new wage-weighted lottery:
- The retention argument is even stronger
- An employer who already has an H-1B employee at Level III/IV has avoided the selection risk
- Replacing that employee means starting over with the lottery — and a 50%+ probability of not getting selected at all in the next lottery cycle
- This creates a retention premium that is now quantifiable as lottery-risk avoidance

Framing: "You've already cleared the lottery selection for my H-1B. Replacing me means going through the new weighted lottery at whatever level the replacement is hired — with no guarantee of selection. That creates a meaningful cost to turnover that justifies investing in retention."

===

KEY CAVEATS AND LIMITATIONS

1. LEGAL CHALLENGE RISK: Final rules can be challenged in court. Monitor immigration news for any injunctions that could delay or block implementation. As of late 2025, the rule was finalized but early 2026 may see legal challenges from employer groups.

2. ANNUAL LOTTERY DESIGN: The weighted system applies at the lottery selection stage. If you are already selected and on an approved H-1B, this rule doesn't retroactively affect your current status.

3. WAGE LEVEL IS NOT JUST ABOUT SALARY: It's tied to the SOC code and duties, not purely the pay rate. A higher salary alone doesn't automatically move you to a higher wage level — the job duties must match the higher level's definition.

4. LOTTERY CAP: The H-1B cap itself (65,000 regular cap + 20,000 master's cap) is unchanged. The weighted system changes who gets selected, not how many are selected total.

===

REFERENCE: WAGE LEVEL DEFINITIONS (DOL)

- Level I: Positions requiring a basic understanding of the occupation. Typically require limited experience.
- Level II: Positions requiring a good understanding, with some complexity in duties performed. Some higher-level knowledge required.
- Level III: Positions requiring a broad range of knowledge applied to complex situations. Work is performed under general supervision.
- Level IV: Positions requiring a high level of knowledge, requiring a complex analysis and decision-making. Work is typically completed with minimal supervision.

For most software engineer and PM roles at major tech companies: Level III–IV is typical. Level I–II tends to correspond to entry-level positions.
