WHAT THE DOL PREVAILING WAGE PROGRAM IS

The Department of Labor sets prevailing wages for H-1B and PERM (green card) applications through the Prevailing Wage Program. Employers must pay H-1B workers at least the prevailing wage for the relevant occupation and location.

The wage is determined by:
1. SOC code (occupation code — e.g., 15-1252 for Software Developers)
2. Geographic area (typically by Metropolitan Statistical Area)
3. Wage level (I through IV, based on job complexity and supervision)

Data source: Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics (OEWS) survey, collected by BLS annually.

Legal authority: Immigration and Nationality Act (INA) Section 212(n); Title 20 CFR Part 655.

===

THE FOUR WAGE LEVELS (DEFINITION)

Level I (Entry):
- Requires only basic understanding of the occupation
- Tasks are routine; works under close supervision
- Limited experience required

Level II (Qualified):
- Good understanding of the occupation; some complexity in duties
- Works under general supervision; makes judgments based on established guidelines
- Some higher-level knowledge required

Level III (Experienced):
- Broad range of knowledge applied to complex situations
- Work performed under general supervision; requires independent judgment
- Tasks involve significant variety and difficulty

Level IV (Fully Competent):
- High level of knowledge; complex analysis and decision-making
- Completed with minimal supervision; often involves instruction of others
- Advanced expertise in the field

Critical distinction: The wage level reflects job complexity and supervision, NOT simply total compensation. A senior developer at Level IV must be doing work that qualifies as "high complexity, minimal supervision" by the DOL definition — the salary alone doesn't determine the level.

===

HOW TO LOOK UP PREVAILING WAGES

Official resource: DOL Foreign Labor Certification Online Wage Library
URL: flcdatacenter.com (FLC Data Center)
Tool: Online Wage Library → select year → select state → select MSA → select SOC code

Also accessible via:
- DOL iCERT system (for employer-submitted LCA lookup)
- OFLC Performance Data (searchable database of past LCA filings with wages paid)

Note: The FLC Data Center shows the OEWS-based wage. Employers can also submit an independent wage survey if they believe the OEWS data is unrepresentative — this is called an "independent authority" wage.

===

WAGE-WEIGHTED LOTTERY IMPLICATIONS (EFFECTIVE FEBRUARY 27, 2026)

Under the finalized H-1B wage-weighted lottery rule (Federal Register 2025-23853):

Lottery entries per wage level:
- Level I: 1 entry
- Level II: 2 entries
- Level III: 3 entries
- Level IV: 4 entries

The salary threshold difference between Level III and Level IV for a Software Developer in San Francisco is approximately $29,890 ($181,210 → $211,100).

Negotiation implication: For an H-1B candidate whose employer would offer anywhere between Level III and Level IV salaries, pushing for Level IV:
1. Increases compensation by ~$30,000/year
2. Changes lottery probability from 3 entries to 4 entries (+33% relative increase in selection probability)
3. Under NFAP analysis, Level IV selection probability increases by 107% vs. the old random lottery

The marginal $30K salary push has compounded expected value: compensation increase × improved lottery odds × career compounding. This is unique to H-1B candidates and does not apply to US citizens.

===

PERM LABOR CERTIFICATION WAGES

For permanent residence (green card) applications, the employer also submits a prevailing wage determination (PWD) through the same system. The PERM prevailing wage uses the same OEWS data but applies to the "offered wage" in the PERM application.

Key difference from H-1B: For PERM, the employer must demonstrate the offered wage meets the prevailing wage at the time of the PERM filing AND at the time the worker receives the visa (which for India EB-2 is 134+ years away in current estimates — making PERM a theoretical rather than practical path for most Indian nationals).

===

EMPLOYER SEARCH: OFLC PERFORMANCE DATA

The DOL OFLC (Office of Foreign Labor Certification) publishes all employer wage certifications. Searchable at: https://www.dol.gov/agencies/eta/foreign-labor/performance

This data shows:
- Exact wages an employer paid for specific H-1B filings
- Occupation codes used for each role
- Geographic areas
- Wage levels certified

Practical use: Before a salary negotiation, look up what the same employer has paid for comparable H-1B filings. If they've filed at $185,000 for a comparable Software Developer role in the same city, you have an employer-disclosed data point for negotiation.
