OVERVIEW: CANADA'S PAY TRANSPARENCY LANDSCAPE

Unlike the US, Canada's pay transparency legislation is at the provincial level (not federal). There is no single national pay transparency law. As of 2025–2026, three provinces have enacted substantive pay transparency requirements; others are at various stages of legislation or consideration.

For salary negotiation purposes, Canadian pay transparency laws are increasingly a candidate's tool: if a job posting must include the salary range, the candidate knows the budget before the first call.

===

BRITISH COLUMBIA: PAY TRANSPARENCY ACT (IN FORCE)

Legislation: Pay Transparency Act, SBC 2023, Chapter 18
Effective: November 1, 2023 (phased implementation by employer size)

What is required:
- All publicly advertised job postings must include the expected salary or wage range
- Applies to any position where the pay can be defined as a range

Phase-in by employer size:
- 1,000+ employees: Required from November 1, 2023
- 300–999 employees: Required from January 1, 2024
- 50–299 employees: Required from January 1, 2025
- All employers (no minimum): Required from January 1, 2026

What is prohibited:
- Employers cannot ask about an applicant's pay history at any point in the hiring process
- Cannot use pay history to set starting wages
- Cannot retaliate against employees who discuss their pay with coworkers

Enforcement: Director of Employment Standards can investigate complaints; penalties can include orders to pay compensation

Salary history ban: YES — BC has a pay history ban built into the Act

===

ONTARIO: WORKING FOR WORKERS FOUR ACT, 2024 (SCHEDULE 2)

Legislation: Working for Workers Four Act, 2024, S.O. 2024, c. 15, Schedule 2
Effective: January 1, 2026 (for job postings)
Threshold: 25+ employees

What is required:
- Job postings for positions in Ontario must include the compensation range (or fixed salary) expected for the role
- Applies to both public-facing and internal job postings visible to current employees

Note on salary history: The Ontario legislation does not include a salary history ban as explicitly as BC. Asking about previous pay remains legally permissible in Ontario.

Enforcement: Employment Standards Officer; failure to comply can result in compliance orders and penalties.

Context: Ontario is Canada's most populous province and largest tech hub (Toronto / Waterloo corridor). This law will affect virtually all major tech employers posting for Ontario-based roles starting January 2026.

===

PRINCE EDWARD ISLAND (PEI): PAY TRANSPARENCY ACT

PEI enacted pay transparency legislation; requires job postings to include wage or salary range. Applies to employers with 10+ employees.

Less directly relevant to tech sector (smaller market) but signals national direction.

===

PENDING / UNDER CONSIDERATION

Alberta: As of 2025, the Alberta government has signaled it is studying pay transparency requirements. No enacted legislation as of mid-2026.

Quebec: Pay equity legislation exists (Act Respecting Pay Equity, focused on gender equity for existing workers), but no specific job posting transparency requirement comparable to BC/Ontario.

Federal public sector: The Pay Equity Act (2021) applies to federally regulated employers (Crown corporations, banks, telecommunications, interprovincial transportation) — focused on gender pay equity between job groups, not individual posting disclosure.

===

NEGOTIATION IMPLICATIONS FOR CANDIDATES IN CANADA

In BC (all employers, as of January 2026) and Ontario (25+ employees, as of January 2026):

1. ANCHORING ADVANTAGE: You know the budget before the call. "I see the range is $140K–$175K; given my [X years] experience and [specific skill], I'm targeting the upper end."

2. REFUSAL TACTIC IS MOOT: In BC, employers cannot ask your previous salary. You never need to deflect; the law prohibits the question. In Ontario, it remains legal to ask, so use the BC deflection scripts if an Ontario employer asks.

3. POSTED RANGE IS A FLOOR, NOT A CEILING: Employers sometimes post ranges lower than their true budget. Negotiating above the posted range is reasonable if you can justify it with market data (from Robert Half Canada, Glassdoor CA, or Levels.fyi Canada data).

4. CANADIAN CONTEXT VS. US CONTEXT: US salary history bans vary by state; Canadian pay transparency is about posting ranges, not banning history questions in every province. When advising a candidate, determine which province they're interviewing in before prescribing tactics.

TECHNICAL SALARY DATA: CANADIAN PAY RANGES IN TECH (OFFICIAL / PUBLIC)

Job Bank Canada (Government of Canada):
- Software Engineers: median CAD $105,000; range CAD $67,000–$160,000
- Computer Systems Analysts: median CAD $90,000; range CAD $60,000–$130,000
- Computer and Information Systems Managers (covers PM roles): median CAD $120,000; range CAD $80,000–$190,000

Source: Government of Canada Job Bank Wage Report (based on LFS / SEPH data)
URL: https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/wagereport

Note: Job Bank Canada uses Statistics Canada data (Survey of Employment, Payrolls and Hours) — the official Canadian equivalent of BLS OEWS data. Like BLS, it captures base salary and may undercount total compensation including bonuses and equity.

===

BC PAY TRANSPARENCY ANNUAL REPORTING (EMPLOYER SIDE)

The BC Pay Transparency Act also requires employers above certain sizes to post annual pay transparency reports showing gender and other pay gap data:
- 1,000+ employees: required from November 2023
- 300–999: required from November 2024
- 50–299: required from November 2025

These reports are filed with the government and may be publicly accessible. In theory, a candidate researching a specific employer could find their pay gap reporting and infer something about internal pay equity.
